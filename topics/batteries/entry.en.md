How can the same battery power light and motion? What is needed to store energy again?

Close the switch and try a filament bulb, LED and motor. Keep the used battery, enter charging and compare absent, incompatible and compatible supplies. Compare two AA chemistries, then zoom from one cell to its pack.

## A complete transport path

Normal discharge requires transport through both the external circuit and the cell. Metal wires already contain mobile electrons. On closure, changing electric fields cause charges around the circuit to respond; the motor need not wait for one electron to finish a lap. Average external electron motion is from negative to positive, opposite conventional current. Opening the switch prevents a sustained conducting path without making stored chemical energy or electrons disappear.

## Two charge carriers and chemical energy

This example follows lithium-ion discharge: oxidation at the negative electrode supplies electrons, reduction at the positive electrode accepts them, and lithium ions move through electrolyte and a porous separator. The separator reduces direct contact between electrodes while permitting ionic transport. Available chemical free energy decreases as electrical energy is supplied externally. Other ions and diffusion also contribute in the electrolyte. Different battery chemistries must not be assumed to share every microscopic detail shown here.

## Loads, charging and battery classification

A current-carrying motor coil experiences magnetic forces that produce torque. Commutation helps sustain rotation, and a real transmission drives the wheels. Energy also becomes heat. A compatible charger supplies external energy to drive the designed reversible reactions back in a rechargeable cell, and cycling is not lossless. Primary cells are not designed for repeated recharging; a similar shape is not evidence that a cell is rechargeable. A heated filament emits light; an LED emits through semiconductor processes, still loses energy as heat, and needs suitable driving and current limiting. Form, chemistry and use are different classification dimensions. Ideal identical cells in series add voltage; parallel branches add amp-hour capacity. Watt-hours account for both, so amp-hours alone cannot compare energy at different voltages.

## A common misconception

A battery is not a jar of electrons that becomes empty. Electrons carry charge; discharge changes the chemical state of materials. An open switch and a depleted battery are different conditions.

## Model limits

This is a qualitative normal low-voltage toy circuit. Particle size, speed, rotation, energy bars and heat allocation are teaching choices, not charge or efficiency measurements. Internal resistance, back EMF, switching transients and self-discharge are omitted. Only net lithium-ion transport is emphasized. Scrubbing and reset do not physically recharge a cell, and the cutaway is not a disassembly guide. The discharge/charge ledger uses normalized energy and illustrative allocations, not product specifications; charging explicitly includes external work and heat. Family specimens are independently fitted, not true-size or capacity comparisons. The pack has at most 16 ideal teaching cells; nominal voltage is not a charging setting.

### Find the little switch gap

Will a toy always turn just because it has a battery inside? Look at the wire connecting the battery, switch and motor. There is still a little gap at the switch. With that path incomplete, the battery cannot keep supplying the toy this way. Close the switch on the screen, then choose Run. We only use the screen controls. A grown-up looks after real batteries.

*Scene cue: Begin with the open circuit, point to the gap, then close it fully before choosing Run once.*

### Different paths outside and inside

Now the blue dots are moving. They stand for electrons in the wire. Look closer inside our battery drawing: the gold dots with plus signs follow an inside path. These are called lithium ions. They are different from electrons. Changes in the battery materials provide energy. The battery is not pouring a jar of blue dots into the motor until the jar becomes empty.

*Scene cue: Run to about 30%; keep the whole circuit as a reference and point to external blue electrons and internal gold lithium ions.*

### Pause and see what changes

Make a guess. If you open the switch now, will the wheels keep turning forever? Try it and take a little time to look. The blue dots stop progressing, the energy bars stop changing, and the wheels stop in this simplified view. The battery’s remaining energy has not suddenly vanished. Close the switch again and we can continue. The part we already used has not been forgotten.

*Scene cue: Open the switch during a run and leave an observation pause. Check preserved progress and energy, then close it again.*

### Store energy again, then meet the family

We still have the battery we used. Visit charging: without an outside energy source, its energy cannot return by itself, and incompatible equipment must not connect. A suitable charger supplies energy. The battery stores some, while some becomes heat. Two AA batteries can look alike yet contain different materials and have different charging rules. A large vehicle pack also needs connections, monitoring and cooling. Grown-ups handle real batteries; we explore only on screen.

*Scene cue: Enter charging with a used battery and compare all three supply conditions. Compare alkaline and NiMH AA specimens, then inspect the pack's three responsibility layers.*
