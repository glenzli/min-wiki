01 · Find the little switch gap
[Begin with the open circuit, point to the gap, then close it fully before choosing Run once.]

Will a toy always turn just because it has a battery inside? Look at the wire connecting the battery, switch and motor. There is still a little gap at the switch. With that path incomplete, the battery cannot keep supplying the toy this way. Close the switch on the screen, then choose Run. We only use the screen controls. A grown-up looks after real batteries.

---

02 · Different paths outside and inside
[Run to about 30%; keep the whole circuit as a reference and point to external blue electrons and internal gold lithium ions.]

Now the blue dots are moving. They stand for electrons in the wire. Look closer inside our battery drawing: the gold dots with plus signs follow an inside path. These are called lithium ions. They are different from electrons. Changes in the battery materials provide energy. The battery is not pouring a jar of blue dots into the motor until the jar becomes empty.

---

03 · Pause and see what changes
[Open the switch during a run and leave an observation pause. Check preserved progress and energy, then close it again.]

Make a guess. If you open the switch now, will the wheels keep turning forever? Try it and take a little time to look. The blue dots stop progressing, the energy bars stop changing, and the wheels stop in this simplified view. The battery’s remaining energy has not suddenly vanished. Close the switch again and we can continue. The part we already used has not been forgotten.

---

04 · Store energy again, then meet the family
[Enter charging with a used battery and compare all three supply conditions. Compare alkaline and NiMH AA specimens, then inspect the pack's three responsibility layers.]

We still have the battery we used. Visit charging: without an outside energy source, its energy cannot return by itself, and incompatible equipment must not connect. A suitable charger supplies energy. The battery stores some, while some becomes heat. Two AA batteries can look alike yet contain different materials and have different charging rules. A large vehicle pack also needs connections, monitoring and cooling. Grown-ups handle real batteries; we explore only on screen.
