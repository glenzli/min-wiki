## Infiltration is not all recharge

Infiltration is entry through the soil surface. Some water stays in unsaturated soil, reaches roots or returns through evaporation; only water that reaches an aquifer becomes groundwater recharge. Runoff and ponding increase when rainfall exceeds infiltration capacity or soil is already saturated, so prior moisture matters.

## Storage space differs from permeability

Porosity measures the fraction of empty space; permeability depends on pore size, connections and fractures. Clay can have many tiny pores yet transmit water slowly. Connected spaces between sand grains often transmit it more readily. Real paving may contain joints or permeable designs; the diagram uses a low-infiltration case. The diagram keeps 100 parcel identities. Water still in the air is not counted at a ground destination; runoff in transit is also distinct from water that has reached the cup.

## How groundwater moves

Most groundwater occupies pores and fractures rather than underground rivers. It moves down hydraulic gradients, sometimes feeding springs and streams or entering wells. Speeds vary widely among formations. Soil can retain or transform some substances, but it cannot guarantee complete purification; clear well water still needs appropriate testing.

## A common misconception

Water entering soil does not instantly become groundwater or automatically become safe to drink.

## Model limits

The 100 parcels and their allocation are a conserved teaching scenario, not measured rain or soil data. Four categories include airborne, infiltrated, runoff and temporarily stored water; cup level counts only arrived runoff. Pores are enlarged, without calculating pressure, capillarity or deep aquifer recharge.

### Rain meets the ground

Have you seen rain soak into a flower bed while making a little stream on a path? The same rain can take different routes on different ground. Let us compare on screen. Choose sand and let the rain fall. Do the blue dots stay on top, move sideways or enter the ground below?

*Scene cue: Play sand from 0%, following the gold drop from the air to the surface.*

### Change the surface

Pause, then compare clay and paving. One hundred little parcels represent the same rain: some are still in the air, some enter the ground, some run away and some stay on top. Add them and no water has vanished. Real results also depend on how hard it rains and how wet the ground was before.

*Scene cue: At 100%, switch sand, clay and paving and compare equal totals with different allocations.*

### Water in tiny spaces

Water underground often fills little spaces between soil grains and rocks, rather like water in the holes of a sponge. Some passages connect easily; others are narrow and water moves slowly. Water that has entered soil may reach plant roots first. It does not all immediately become deep groundwater.

*Scene cue: Scrub to around 55% and inspect the pores, where blue parcels follow pale connected channels.*

### Care for unseen water

Groundwater can slowly reach springs and rivers and continue its journey. But passing through soil does not guarantee that every harmful substance was removed. Clear well water is not something to drink without checking. Remember that the ground beneath us holds many small water pathways, with conditions adults and scientists need to investigate.

*Scene cue: Return to the overview and watch runoff arrive in the cup; the final parcels remain visible.*
