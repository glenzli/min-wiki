01 · Place today in a long history
[Show the linear 4540 Ma overview before the last-million-year window. Do not compare spacing between different scales directly.]

Earth has a history of about four billion five hundred and forty million years. Our species occupies a tiny sliver beside today on that ruler. Enlarging it reveals details without changing how much time really passed.

02 · Rocks contain natural clocks
[Compare meteorite, terrestrial-mineral and lunar dating evidence, distinguishing ancient uranium-lead applications from recent carbon-14 dating.]

Some atoms change according to predictable patterns. Scientists choose suitable atomic clocks to investigate minerals and rocks, then combine different evidence. No single birthday stone preserves Earth's entire formation.

---

03 · Ask where the oxygen goes
[Keep production while comparing two consumption conditions. Emphasize arbitrary units and net retention, not a reconstructed ancient oxygen curve.]

Some ancient microbes used sunlight to make food and release oxygen. Living processes and chemical reactions also consumed it. Greater production or lower consumption can leave more behind. This change took a long time and included fluctuations.

---

04 · Different life changes the same world
[Compare animal diversification, land transitions and five crises among the 28 evidence landmarks. Distinguish rounded event ages, boundaries and durations, then connect the Life, Rocks and Climate halls.]

Air, oceans and land keep changing. Life changes environments too: some ways of living expand while others become restricted. Animals, forests and humans each occupy only part of this long history. Earth's past is not a staircase arranged toward one destination.
