Beach sand feels fine, but its grains may have different stories. Some were part of rocks; others came from hard shells. Choose a source and notice its color and shape. Today we follow one little grain, almost as if we had a magnifying glass to accompany it on part of its journey.

Over long periods rocks can crack and change, leaving fragments. Water carries some away. Collisions and rubbing during the journey can make them smaller or rounder. Our picture moves quickly, but nature may take a long time. Pause and compare the grain in the circle with the one you saw at the beginning.

Now choose a shell and watch again. Shells can break too, and nearby currents can carry their fragments around. They need not visit a mountain before returning by river. One beach can contain grains from many places. Looking at color alone does not tell us exactly what every grain is made of.

When transport conditions change, some grains settle beside others to form the sand we see. This may only be a stop along the way: later waves or wind can move them again. When you examine sand with an adult, avoid throwing it or rubbing your eyes. Enjoy finding its many little colors and shapes.
