Find blue-marked W1. The two elbowed structures on its head are antennae, not legs. Count three legs on each side of the middle region: six altogether. Locate the mandibles by the mouth. We will follow this same worker outside.

At first there is no purple trail for this food. W1 searches and eventually finds it. On returning, it marks only places already passed. Purple makes an invisible chemical clue visible; real scent is not a string of beads. Nestmates then join this illustrative recruitment.

Weak clues can lead to more searching; experienced W1 may recover a direction. A stone is different: scent cannot let an ant walk through it. Watch W1 explore above it and mark a new path on return. This is one possible success, not a guaranteed answer.

W1 brings food to the same nest. N1 is a different care worker, and L1 is a separate young individual. Review its egg, larval, pupal and new-adult record. Development takes far longer than a food trip. The queen lays eggs, rather than directing workers with a map.
