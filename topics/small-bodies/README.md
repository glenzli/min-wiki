# 小行星、彗星与尘埃 / Asteroids, comets and dust

This topic owns its scientific state, hypothetical orbit, representative surfaces, finite 3D scenes, controls, bilingual reading and tests. Reader owns presentation, language/navigation and delivery. No other topic's UI or scientific model is imported.

## Learning contract

1. **Question:** how do shape, shading and composition differ? **Variable:** source direction on one retained body, plus contrasting samples. **Observation:** the terminator moves while the same geometry and sample identity remain. **Cause:** illumination changes reflected brightness, not composition. **Likely misconception:** color or size alone defines composition or dwarf-planet status. **Evidence/boundary:** NASA asteroid, Ceres, dwarf-planet and Psyche pages; original representative surfaces, independently fitted screen sizes, no inferred interior.
2. **Question:** where do comet tails come from and point? **Variables:** one comet's elapsed orbital phase and presence of exposed ice. **Observation:** distance, inverse-square sunlight, qualitative activity, coma and two tail directions change together; no ice produces no modeled activity without moving the orbit. **Cause:** heating → sublimation and entrained dust → coma → solar-wind/field effects and radiation-pressure/retained-motion cues. **Likely misconception:** a burning tail simply trails motion. **Boundary:** defined hypothetical Kepler ellipse, not a real ephemeris; qualitative response and curves, not thermal or particle dynamics.
3. **Question:** how do sparse reservoirs connect to dust and meteors? **Variables:** reservoir/camera and Earth's position around a representative coplanar stream. **Observation:** disk versus inferred spherical structure, with an Earth halo only during a widened stream crossing. **Cause:** released debris remains in space; atmospheric entry, rather than merely being dust in space, can produce a meteor. **Likely misconception:** dense rock walls, one sharply bounded dust belt, a predicted meteor shower, or a photographed Oort cloud. **Boundary:** representative points, adjusted widths, separate Oort structure scale, no measured number density or dates.

## Files and lifetimes

- `model.ts`: deterministic route parsing, hypothetical Kepler orbit (`a=3.2 AU`, `e=.72`), inverse-square solar flux, qualitative exposed-ice response, anti-solar ion curve, lagged dust curve, same-orbit Earth/stream condition, stable representative point samples and surface relief.
- `scene.ts`: one Three.js renderer with bounded retained groups. Four surface meshes use 96×64 sphere grids; tail point buffers have 420+850 points; reservoir samples total 1,610 points, plus 400 stream points and 220 decorative stars. Geometry is not regenerated on ordinary frames. Tail buffer positions are resampled on state change. Samples are not measured positions, shape models or physical particle counts.
- `main.ts`: one user-started finite clock, three retained scientific views, URL projection, independent camera controls, failure/retry and shared Reader presentation/reading adapters. Defaults are stationary. Hiding or leaving stops playback; restored visibility does not jump elapsed time or auto-resume. Reduced motion replaces playback with explicit steps. OrbitControls damping is disabled, and paused scenes render only on changes. No external textures, Workers or network requests are used by the scene.
- `study.ts`, `learning.json`, `locales/en.json`, `reader.html`: equivalent Chinese/English scientific conditions and four narration sections. Downloaded narration excludes cues. Markdown retains a manual reading extension outside the generated learning region.
- `fallback.svg`: original static vector illustration, deliberately identified as a fixed diagram rather than the current experiment frame. A WebGL creation/render/context failure retains controls, readouts, explanation, navigation and a retry button. Disposal releases geometries/materials, controls, observer, DOM labels and renderer. No textures require disposal.

Camera pose is remembered separately for shapes, orbit, close nucleus, each reservoir, and scientific state. Selecting another view pauses the clock and retains both. Close tracking follows the same nucleus; rotating that view does not modify phase. URL state restores scientific conditions after refresh, while hand-adjusted camera poses are session-only. Focused canvas keyboard controls: arrows rotate, +/− zoom, Home resets. Touch gestures use OrbitControls; controls also use native keyboard semantics.

## Public route parameters

- `?view=shapes&sample=carbon|silicate|metal|ceres&light=-150..150`
- `?view=comet&orbit=0..1&ice=1|0&lens=orbit|nucleus`: 0 = aphelion, .5 = perihelion, 1 = next aphelion; phase measures elapsed orbital time, not true anomaly.
- `?view=belts&region=main|kuiper|dust|oort&earth=0..1`
- `labels=0|1` controls projected object names. Reader `lang`/`reading`, deployment prefix and anchors are preserved. Unknown enums use defaults; finite numeric input is clamped.

## Scale, evidence and source ownership

The comet's Sun and nucleus are enlarged independently from orbital distances, and tails/coma are separately enlarged illustrations. The main-belt, Kuiper and inner-dust views use linear AU geometry with different camera zooms; marker radii are not diameters. Sample radial bounds 2.1–3.3 AU, 30–50 AU and .45–4.4 AU define this illustration's point generator, not sharp physical boundaries. The dust cloud is only an inner-system excerpt, and the associated stream uses this same hypothetical comet ellipse. The Oort Cloud is a separately scaled proposed structure with no common length conversion. Its point positions are not observations.

Specific sources are linked with the associated scene and in `learning.json`: NASA [asteroids](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/facts/), [Ceres](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/ceres/facts/), [dwarf planets](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/), [Psyche composition](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/16-psyche/), [comets](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/facts/), [Kuiper Belt](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/kuiper-belt/facts/), [Oort Cloud](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/oort-cloud/facts/), [interplanetary dust](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/10-things-dust-in-the-wind-on-mars-and-well-beyond/), [meteors](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/facts/), ESA [comet structure](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2023/11/Structure_of_a_comet), and JPL's [Oort inference and Solar System structure lesson](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/lesson-plan/modeling-the-structure-of-the-solar-system/).
