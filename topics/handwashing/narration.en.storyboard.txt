01 · Busy little hands
[Hold the wet stage and show both hands.]

Hands have busy days: touching toys, turning pages, opening doors and holding food. Some things on them are visible, while microbes are too small to see. We do not need to fear every tiny thing, but careful washing reduces the chance of moving harmful microbes to our mouth, eyes or nose. Start with clean running water.

---

02 · Soap helps
[Advance soap and rubbing, then pause for three seconds.]

Apply soap or wash-off liquid hand soap. Soap helps water carry away oily material, while rubbing helps loosen what is attached. The little heads and tails in our picture represent molecules, not giant bubbles. Pause and watch the model dirt change from sticking to the skin to becoming loosened.

---

03 · Reach every area
[Select all five regions while the marker moves between them.]

Wash both hands carefully. Rub the palms and backs, reach between fingers, turn around each thumb, and include fingertips and nail areas. Scrub with soap for at least twenty seconds and cover everything. Our buttons help you remember places; however quickly you tap them, they cannot do the real washing for your hands.

---

04 · Rinse and dry
[Advance to rinsing, compare the two imagined methods at the same patch, return to the soap route, then dry. Do not treat drawn residue as a test result.]

Rinse away soap and loosened dirt with clean running water, then dry with a clean towel or suitable hand-drying equipment. Washed hands are not completely microbe-free, but washing reduces opportunities for spread. Remember the sequence and practice it with an adult before eating and after using the toilet.
