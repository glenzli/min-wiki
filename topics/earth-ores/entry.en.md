## Why are mineral, rock, ore and gem overlapping descriptions? {#materials}

They answer different questions. A small specimen can fit several descriptions at once.

| What are you asking? | Concept | Example and qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is its composition and internal structure? | Mineral species | Quartz SiO₂, chalcopyrite CuFeS₂ and cassiterite SnO₂ are different minerals. Ordered structure does not require every grain to have an attractive outline. |
| How are geological materials combined, and how did they form? | Rock | Granite combines several minerals; bauxite also commonly mixes minerals as rock material. |
| Can useful constituents be extracted under given conditions? | Ore | Content, mineralogy, processing, scale and costs matter. Copper-bearing material is not automatically mineable copper ore. |
| What decorative use and quality requirements does it meet? | Gem material | Some quartz is used as gems, while other quartz is industrial feed or an ordinary grain. Its mineral name need not change. |

Gangue is not a mineral name either. Quartz may accompany the target minerals as gangue in a copper-processing route; under another use and quality requirement, quartz itself can be the target material. **Usefulness, recoverability and suitability for extraction require separate judgments.** [USGS on ore](https://www.usgs.gov/communications-and-publishing/news/earthword-ore) and [Smithsonian on minerals, rocks and gems](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/earth-science/gems-and-minerals-beauties-and-building-blocks) provide the conceptual background.

This gallery develops three representative paths: hydrothermal veins, residual bauxite and running-water cassiterite placers. They do not classify every deposit. Magmatic, sedimentary, replacement, evaporative and other processes form additional mineral resources. Across the three paths, keep asking: **What moved, what remained, and did any material appear from nowhere?**

## Path one: why can fluid leave a mineral vein in a fracture? {#vein}

### Hydrothermal fluid is not a stream of molten metal

Water-rich fluids can carry dissolved siliceous and metal-bearing components. Heat sources, fluid origins and the rocks encountered influence their chemistry. Different systems can involve magmatic, metamorphic, sedimentary and groundwater processes. Hot water under pressure need not boil like water at the surface; there is no single formation temperature for all ore deposits. [Museum Wales: hydrothermal sources, chemistry and physical conditions](https://museum.wales/mineralogy-of-wales/hydrothermal-minerals/)

Separating the prerequisites reveals places where the process can stop:

1. **A material supply.** Fluid can obtain components from rocks it encounters or other sources. The rock surrounding the displayed specimen need not be the only source of its metals.
2. **An accessible pathway.** Fractures, faults or permeable layers permit migration. Drawing a pale line alone has not explained how material arrived.
3. **Conditions allowing precipitation or reaction.** Cooling, pressure change, mixing, boiling or wall-rock reaction can make some minerals remain. Different minerals require different conditions.
4. **Preservation of the record.** Later deformation, weathering, erosion or another fluid episode can change what survives.

A vein may fill a fracture or retain cavities into which crystals grow. The same geological system can also contain many small veins or dispersed fine mineralization. Large attractive crystals are only one possible appearance that may survive and be selected for display. [Museum Wales: contrasting hydrothermal occurrences](https://museum.wales/mineralogy-of-wales/hydrothermal-minerals/)

## Path two: how can the remainder become relatively richer in aluminum without adding it? {#bauxite}

### Follow the parent rock and the exported fraction

Some bauxites form through intense weathering of aluminum-bearing parent material and water-mediated export of relatively mobile components. Aluminum-bearing minerals and some iron- and titanium-bearing material remain relatively behind, changing the remainder's composition. New minerals also form during real weathering; retaining provenance does not mean retaining every original crystal intact.

Suitable parent material, chemistry, drainage and landscape history must work together. Tropical red soil is not automatically bauxite, and granite is not its only possible parent. For example, USGS documents bauxite formed through **intense in-place weathering of syenite** in Arkansas. This is one specific relationship, rather than a universal recipe. [USGS: geochemistry of Arkansas bauxite and associated rocks](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geochemical-analyses-bauxite-and-associated-rocks-arkansas-bauxite-region-central-arkansas)

The gallery's profile represents one residual path. Some bauxites also involve transport, sedimentation or karst settings. One teaching profile cannot describe every bauxite's complete history. [Australian Museum: residual and other deposit paths](https://australian.museum/learn/minerals/geological-deposits/geological-ore-deposits/)

### Retain both number and proportion in the budget

The exercise starts with thirty arbitrary source shares: twelve aluminum-bearing, six other relatively retained and twelve relatively mobile tags.

- Weaker weathering makes less material available for export.
- Weathering without effective removal of dissolved components does not produce the same enrichment as the strongly leached case.
- Under stronger weathering and effective export, the twelve relatively mobile tags leave the profile but remain recorded outside it. The original twelve aluminum-bearing tags have not multiplied.

If the remainder falls from thirty tags to eighteen, the aluminum-bearing fraction changes from 12/30 to 12/18. **This is a tag-count example, not an increase from 40% to 66.7% elemental aluminum or alumina grade.** Tags have no calibrated masses. Ignoring the other retained material would falsely turn the remainder into pure aluminum.

### Red-brown color, rounded grains and the name bauxite

Bauxite may be earthy, massive or contain rounded pisolitic/oolitic grains. Every specimen need not share one appearance. Iron oxides and hydroxides often contribute red-brown colors, which do not directly measure aluminum.

Bauxite commonly contains gibbsite, boehmite and diaspore, with varying siliceous material, iron oxides, titanium-bearing material and clays. **It is mixed material, not one mineral species or already-refined aluminum metal.** Mineralogy, extractable components and impurities affect processing suitability. [USGS: heterogeneous bauxite composition and uses](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/bauxite-and-alumina-statistics-and-information)

## Path three: why can denser cassiterite remain in one flow and move away in another? {#placer}

### Liberate grains before comparing their individual behavior

Cassiterite can first form in primary rock or veins, then become loose through weathering and erosion. Rivers and other transport can redistribute grains and concentrate them locally under suitable conditions. Primary deposits and placers are related but different occurrences. [USGS: primary tin mineralization, placers and uses](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/tin)

If minerals remain locked in parent rock, the transported object is a composite fragment. Its constituents cannot be treated as independent grains yet. This gallery makes liberation a prerequisite, then holds representative grain size equal while comparing six cassiterite and eighteen quartz grains.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Not liberated | All twenty-four grains remain in source rock | This is the locked-condition contrast, not a claim that natural water cannot erode rock. |
| Liberated, weak transport | The assigned selective transport has not been completed | No enrichment is a meaningful outcome too. |
| Liberated, selective transport | Six cassiterite grains remain in the low part of the window; eighteen quartz grains leave it | The case isolates a property contrast at equal representative size, without specifying a universal velocity threshold. |
| Liberated, strong transport | Both kinds move beyond the window | Heavy minerals are not immovable; all twenty-four grains still have recorded destinations. |

Typical cassiterite SnO₂ density is about 6.98–7.01 g/cm³, versus about 2.65 g/cm³ for quartz. This supports a mass comparison at equal representative volume. Natural size, shape, bed conditions, turbulence, cohesion and repeated floods also influence entrainment, settling and transport. **A large quartz grain and a tiny cassiterite grain cannot be ranked for every motion using mineral density alone.** The diagram does not calculate flow thresholds from these reference densities. [Cassiterite data](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/cassiterite.pdf), [quartz data](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/quartz.pdf)

Dark and light are tracking codes here. Natural dark sands can contain several heavy minerals, and cassiterite has more than one color. Dark sand alone guarantees neither tin nor a measured grade or extraction value.

## What happens between natural occurrence and a displayed specimen? {#specimen}

### Parent rock, wall rock, vein and cavity describe different relationships

Aluminum-bearing parent rock supplies the starting material in the residual case. Wall rock surrounds a vein. Hydrothermal components may come from elsewhere, so proximity alone does not make the wall rock the source of every metal. Fracture veins, cavity linings and interlocking grains also describe different spatial arrangements.

With open growing space, some crystals develop natural faces into a cavity. Constrained grains can retain ordered internal structure without complete outward forms. A cavity need not contain economically useful metal, and a vein need not be full of pointed crystals. Continue to [Minerals and crystals](../earth-minerals/entry.en.md) to compare these occurrence sections.

### Exposing, cleaning and cutting change different things

A natural outcrop records erosion and geological position. Professional sampling records location and rock relationships. Removing attached material, exposing a mineral by removing some host rock, cutting and polishing are distinct treatments; specimens do not all pass through the same sequence. Cutting can create a new flat surface. A natural growth face has a different history.

The 3D specimen here is an original representative vein sample, without a measured locality or scan. **The cutaway changes visibility while retaining branches and grains at A; it does not simulate extraction, dissolution or crystal growth.** Spatial textures help read relationships without serving as an assay. Actual display specimens can also be selected for a particular texture or appearance, rather than representing an ore body's average material. [Smithsonian: the different research roles of ore and mineral collections](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/mineral-sciences/collections-overview)

## How far does each clue take us? Reveal evidence in layers {#evidence}

| Evidence layer | What does it help answer? | What does it leave unresolved? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occurrence, cross-cutting, weathering profiles and sediment layers | Location, rock relationships and some relative sequence | Exact age, mineral identity and economic extent do not follow automatically. |
| Color, luster, grains and intergrowths | Descriptions of visible assemblages and textures | Similar appearances need not share an identity; a photograph lacks many properties. |
| Typical hardness, streak, cleavage and density | Comparisons with independent properties, including conflicting candidates | A mineral's data do not characterize every mixed ore, and similar densities do not establish identical minerals. |
| Microscopy, mineralogy, diffraction and chemistry | Grain relationships, crystalline phases and elemental composition | An element's presence alone does not identify its mineral carrier; each method has limits. |
| Representative samples across locations and processing trials | Distribution, grade variation, impurities, liberation and recovery | One rich specimen cannot replace sampling, scale and feasibility work for an entire body. |

For example, chalcopyrite CuFeS₂ may have a brass-yellow metallic appearance. Typical Mohs hardness is about 3.5–4, streak green-black, and density 4.1–4.3 g/cm³. Pyrite can also look metallic yellow while being a different mineral. Cassiterite's hardness is about 6–7, and its appearance varies from dark material to paler, more transparent forms. **Reference data describe minerals; they are not chemical tests performed on colored areas of the screen.** [Chalcopyrite data](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/chalcopyrite.pdf), [cassiterite data](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/cassiterite.pdf)

X-ray diffraction helps identify crystalline phases; chemical analyses constrain elements; microscopy and elemental maps show where components lie within grains. A USGS deposit study combines microscopy, diffraction and fluorescence because no one observation answers every question. The gallery borrows that complementary-evidence approach, not the study site's measurements. [USGS: an actual multi-method study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/combining-scanning-electron-microscopy-x-ray-diffraction-and-x-ray-fluorescence)

Family exploration can stay on screen and in existing reference information. Do not taste, powder or acid-test unknown specimens. Streak and hardness here are virtual evidence rather than home-processing instructions.

## High content, recoverability and extraction value are different questions {#budget}

First identify what is being weighed. A target metal's mass fraction, the mass fraction of a mineral and content reported as an oxide are different numbers. Bauxite's aluminum-bearing tag fraction is not Al₂O₃ grade. A window containing only cassiterite is not 100% tin metal.

The economic bench separately sets a **hypothetical target-metal grade**. It does not automatically convert the colors of 36, thirty or twenty-four tags into an assay. The bench shares the same one-tonne feed function as the Minerals hall:

- Feed is fixed at 1 tonne = 1000 kg.
- Contained target metal = 1000 × grade% ÷ 100.
- Recoverable target metal = contained amount × recovery% ÷ 100.
- Token revenue = recoverable amount × token value per kg of target metal; token balance = revenue − cost.

| Hold grade at 1%, recovery at 80% and cost at 90 tokens | Recoverable target metal | Revenue | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Value of 8 tokens/kg | 8 kg | 64 tokens | −26 tokens |
| Change only value to 15 tokens/kg | Still 8 kg | 120 tokens | +30 tokens |

The balance changed without creating metal in the specimen. In the first row, feed contains 10 kg of target metal, of which 8 kg is recovered and 2 kg is unrecovered; the original feed also contains much accompanying material. Feed minus recovered metal is an idealized remainder, **not an actual tailings-mass forecast**, because plant water, reagents and other inputs are absent.

Real evaluation also considers extent, continuity, impurities, infrastructure, technical performance and other factors. Tokens are not market prices, reserve estimates, investment findings or permission to mine. They show why one number cannot replace a set of conditions. ## Environmental questions do not disappear into the token balance {#environment}

The three paths connect different Earth systems, and extraction or processing can have different environmental effects.

- **Sulfur-bearing minerals and water.** Some sulfide weathering in oxygen, water and suitable conditions can generate acidic drainage and mobilize metals. Mineralogy, acid generation and neutralization, hydrology and management matter. Mine waters are not all equally acidic or contaminated. [USGS: mine drainage](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/mine-drainage)
- **Bauxite and processing residues.** Extraction changes land. Alumina-processing residues such as red mud also raise high-pH, storage and water-management questions. Alkaline residues differ from possible acidic drainage; one color or pollution score cannot capture both. [US EPA: bauxite and alumina production wastes](https://www.epa.gov/radiation/tenorm-bauxite-and-alumina-production-wastes)
- **Placers, rivers and seafloors.** Removing sediment can increase suspended material and disturb beds and ecosystems. A grain-sorting diagram does not replace real river-process evidence or environmental assessment. [USGS: tin and environmental connections](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/tin)

Follow land, water, energy and wastes separately, and ask whether products can be repaired, used longer or recycled. [Earth history](../earth-history/entry.en.md) and [Ice and climate](../earth-climate/entry.en.md) compare different time scales. Geological formation, daily use and environmental management do not share one simple countdown.

## Deeper questions: separate the scales in the demonstration {#questions}

### Does 50% progress mean half the mineralization time has passed?

No. Progress arranges this exercise's stages on a draggable axis without a calibrated age. Repeated hydrothermal pulses, prolonged weathering and recurring river transport can have very different durations and interruptions. The three cases' halfway points represent neither equal time nor equal material transfer. Real timing requires evidence such as local stratigraphy, ages and process rates, which this diagram does not supply.

### Does retaining a source tag mean an old crystal never changed?

No. A tag remembers where material came from, but real material can dissolve and enter a new mineral. Retaining the same aluminum source is compatible with changes in mineral species, water content and structure. Treating each tag as an unchanged whole mineral would conceal weathering and precipitation mechanisms. It is not a countable atom or molecule either.

### Can a real vein reverse as easily as the 3D cutaway?

The slider controls visibility. Restoring the cover retains spatial relationships at A so that sections and exterior can be compared. It does not reattach cut rock or reverse erosion, extraction or mineralization. To test whether formation occurs, change supply, access and progress in the process view. Use the cutaway to inspect internal relationships.

### If only cassiterite remains in the window, is it 100% tin?

No. First, these are representative grain counts without measured masses. Second, cassiterite SnO₂ contains tin and oxygen, rather than being pure tin metal. A real sample can also contain other minerals, inclusions and locked grains. Grain fraction, cassiterite mass content and tin-metal grade each require a stated denominator, method and representative sampling. One color readout cannot replace those three questions.

### If chemistry detects copper, why inspect minerals and texture?

Which elements occur is a different question from their mineral carriers and grain relationships. Different minerals can carry copper, and intergrowths can affect liberation and recovery. Diffraction, microscopy and chemistry provide complementary evidence; one analysis point also cannot replace representative samples across locations. Further evidence may reveal conflicts, rather than merely supporting an initial guess.

### If tokens turn positive, has the rock become proven mineable ore?

Only one idealized budget condition changed. At fixed grade and recovery, changing value or cost creates no metal and supplies no deposit extent, geological continuity, real processing performance or environmental evidence. Real ore judgments depend on technical and economic conditions. A positive teaching balance has not completed that assessment or established reserves or permission to mine.

## Specimen identity and resource judgment need different evidence and budgets

A mineral species concerns composition and structure; rock describes an aggregate and its formation; ore concerns useful constituents extractable under particular technical and economic conditions; gem concerns use and quality. These criteria overlap. Bauxite is mixed material, not one mineral species. Appearance, texture, hardness, streak and density provide different clues, while microscopy, diffraction, chemistry and representative sampling answer further questions. Mineral data do not directly characterize mixed ore, and one rich specimen cannot establish an entire body's average grade. The teaching budget fixes 1000 kg of feed: contained target = 1000 × grade% / 100; recovered amount then multiplies recovery% / 100. Token revenue minus cost compares assumptions only. It estimates neither real reserves nor processing routes, environmental acceptability or permission. Remaining feed is not a complete real-tailings forecast because water, reagents and other inputs are excluded. Land, water, energy, wastes and ecosystems need separate assessment; possible sulfide drainage acidity and alumina-residue alkalinity are different chemical issues.

## A common misconception

A white vein does not guarantee gold, metallic luster does not prove gold, and dark sand does not prove tin. Quartz can occur without the selected metal mineralization. Relative aluminum enrichment does not manufacture aluminum, and strong enough flow can move heavy minerals too. A beautiful cavity is no proof of deposit value, and a flat cut is not a natural crystal face. Tag fractions, mineral content, elemental grades and oxide grades are not interchangeable. A positive token balance does not establish that real mining should proceed.

### Heavy grains can move too

Minerals must first separate from their parent rock before we compare individual grains in water. In this equal-representative-size exercise, selective transport moves quartz farther while denser cassiterite remains. Weak flow has not completed sorting; stronger transport can move both kinds beyond the window. Leaving the observation window does not mean disappearing. Real rivers also depend on grain size, shape and the bed.

### From a specimen to daily life

Copper can enter wires, aluminum can enter packaging, and tin can enter solder. Finding their minerals does not mean that retrieving them is easy. We need more evidence and must compare content, recoverable amounts and costs, as well as land, water, energy and wastes. Using objects longer, repairing them and recycling continue the ore story into our own lives.
