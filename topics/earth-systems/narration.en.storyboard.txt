01 · Compare equal volumes of water
[Compare equal-volume A and B; vary temperature and salinity separately and together, including a nearly equal-density case.]

Two equal-sized seawater samples can have different masses and therefore different densities. Temperature and salt both matter. If colder water is also much fresher, compare both changes together.

02 · The surface and deep ocean connect
[Read surface-deep connections, identifying North Atlantic and Southern Ocean regions and return mechanisms without promising a unique closed trajectory.]

Winds drive many surface currents. In some places, cooling and increasing salt contribute to deep-water formation. Mixing and upwelling help water return. The ocean has many branches, rather than one pipe wrapped around Earth.

---

03 · Why night arrives and auroras shine
[Follow one ground station through daylight, then show field and particle explanations. Hiding a layer does not remove a real physical condition.]

As Earth turns, the same ground faces toward and away from the Sun. Auroras high above have another cause: charged particles transfer energy to particles in the air, which emit light. A dark sky simply makes the glow easier to see.

04 · One what-if needs several kinds of evidence
[Compare evidence and unknowns for all six conditions. Label inverse-square readings as relative sunlight, without a habitability score.]

Closer to the Sun, Earth receives more sunlight, but that is not yet a surface temperature. Air, oceans, rocks, gravity and other conditions shape the environment together. When one condition changes, explain what is known and what remains uncertain.
