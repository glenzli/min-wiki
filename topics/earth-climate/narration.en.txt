A long Cryogenian glaciation. Glacial evidence from low latitudes helped motivate research into Snowball Earth. Large ice sheets repeatedly grew and shrank within a long-term icehouse. We live in a warmer interglacial, not on an Earth without ice.

Keep sunlight on the same surface patch fixed. Change only the fraction covered by bright ice and snow, and follow the same 100 portions of sunlight. Positive feedback amplifies an initial change; positive does not mean beneficial. The reverse can amplify cooling as ice grows and reflection increases. Climate also radiates heat outward and responds to greenhouse gases, clouds, ocean storage and transport. This reflection budget cannot output surface temperature, freezing thresholds or the date of another glaciation.

A glacier stores water: snowfall adds to it; melt and other ablation remove it. Even a cold place can lose ice if supply is small. Hold one condition fixed and change the other. Accumulation adds glacier mass through snowfall and other processes; ablation removes it through melting, sublimation, calving and more. Here they are reduced to two controlled quantities. Real glaciers flow downhill, and terminus positions lag climate. Losing mass does not mean ice stops moving. Persistent negative balance is more informative than one weather event.

Equal water-equivalent masses in a freshwater approximation. Floating ice already displaced its own weight of water, so its direct melt effect is approximately zero. Land ice adds water to the ocean. Water-level changes are exaggerated and are not real meters. Real sea level also responds to thermal expansion, salinity, currents, gravity and land motion. Density and salinity corrections make floating ice's direct effect different from exact mathematical zero. Losing floating ice shelves can also reduce restraint on land-ice flow and raise sea level indirectly. We separate two main mechanisms; sea-ice loss is not harmless.
