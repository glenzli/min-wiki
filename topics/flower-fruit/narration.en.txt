A flower on a branch may later produce a cherry. Which part becomes the fruit? Before guessing the petals, look inside. Find the ovary and the ovules it contains. These are new names, but you only need to remember their positions and follow what develops from them.

A visiting bee may help pollen reach the right place. That is pollination. Pause and find where it arrived. Compatible pollen still needs to grow a tube so reproductive cells can reach an ovule for fertilisation. One bee visit therefore does not guarantee a fruit.

As petals fall, a young fruit grows. Cherry flesh develops from part of the ovary, while an ovule develops into a seed. They do not become the same thing. The cutaway shows fruit around a hard stone, with the actual seed inside the stone.

The cherry fills out and changes colour as the plant supplies materials over time. Our few screen steps compress a much longer process. When eating cherries with a grown-up, inspect the flesh and stone and remember the flower. Other fruits can involve different floral parts, so they do not all follow cherry anatomy.
