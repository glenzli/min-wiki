How can a flowering plant reach its next generation, and which steps can fail?

Change the bean’s water, air and temperature and scrub its growth album. Compare compatible and incompatible cherry pollen, then inspect dandelion dispersal or burdock contact, transport and landing. Watch the condition map: arrival is not germination, and pollination is not fertilization.

## Germination is conditional renewed growth

A healthy common bean resumes metabolism after taking up water. Its radicle emerges, its hypocotyl lifts the cotyledons, and true leaves unfold. Water, oxygen and suitable temperature are the conditions represented here; flooding can limit oxygen supply. Changing to dry soil does not turn existing structures back into a seed: later growth pauses. Early growth uses stored reserves, while an established seedling needs light, water, mineral nutrients and sufficient growth to become reproductively mature. Seedling-to-flowering growth is not simulated, and the bean never becomes a cherry.

## Pollination, fertilization and fruit are different events

The cherry comparison begins with a flower on a mature tree. Pollen arriving on the stigma is pollination. A pollen tube then grows through the style before sperm can reach an ovule and fertilization can occur. The compatible case assumes other conditions are suitable; incompatible pollen-tube growth is arrested and no seeds or fruit develop in that case. A fertilized ovule usually develops into a seed; the ovary becomes the fruit and its wall becomes the pericarp. Cherry flesh and stone are pericarp: the stone itself is not a seed. This teaching branch is not a yield prediction across cultivars, pollen quantities or environments.

## Dispersal still leaves an establishment hurdle

The dandelion comparison shows a pappus-bearing small fruit settling while wind carries it sideways. Burdock’s hooked bracts first contact fur, then remain attached during transport before release and falling. These are different species, not later forms of a cherry. Each dispersal unit keeps its identity, but landing never creates an automatic seedling: viability, dormancy, environment and species-specific requirements still affect germination and establishment. The condition map illustrates a typical sexual reproductive pathway, not every instance of apomixis, vegetative reproduction or seedless fruit.

## A common misconception

Not every landed seed germinates, and not every pollinated flower produces fruit. A dandelion pappus carries a small fruit; a cherry seed is inside its stone. The comparison cases are not one plant changing species.

## Model limits

The bean is a healthy-seed conditional album, with cold or oxygen limitation represented by pausing later growth. Cherry offers illustrative compatible-success and incompatible-blocked cases, not computed fruit-set probabilities. Dispersal paths and all stage durations are not measurement-calibrated; wind fields and germination rates are not calculated. Cases keep separate state and pause on switching; scrubbing revisits a record rather than reversing plant growth.

### Suitable starting conditions

A bean holds a tiny new plant. Water helps it become active again, but staying underwater is not good: it also needs oxygen. With suitable conditions, a little root usually appears first.

*Scene cue: Compare dry, flooded and damp conditions at the bean’s starting state, then restore conditions and observe the root.*

### A seedling does not flower immediately

The shoot rises and true leaves unfold. It still needs light, water, nutrients and more growth before it can flower. Next we move to an already mature cherry tree. This is a different comparison case.

*Scene cue: Keep the bean’s true-leaf state, note that growth to maturity is not simulated, then switch to cherry with an explicit species change.*

### Arriving pollen is not enough

A bee brings pollen to the flower. A compatible pollen tube can keep growing before fertilization may occur. Sometimes pollen arrives without succeeding. When a cherry develops, its seed is hidden inside the stone.

*Scene cue: Compare compatible and incompatible cases in the same cherry cutaway, noting the arrested tube and absence of fruit.*

### Ask again after landing

Two other plants show different journeys: dandelion fruits ride the wind and burdock hooks catch on animal fur. Reaching a new place does not automatically make a seedling. Ask again: are water, air and temperature suitable here?

*Scene cue: Observe wind-borne landing and burdock contact, transport and release. Read the condition map and return to the retained bean comparison.*
