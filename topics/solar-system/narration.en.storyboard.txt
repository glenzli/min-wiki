01 · Meet the family
[Show the initial orbit overview.]

Let’s begin beside the Sun. The Sun makes its own light, and eight planets travel around it. Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars are closer in. Farther out are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. You do not have to remember every name yet. Choose a world you like and find its path.

---

02 · What will we meet below?
[Pause Earth’s descent at the globe, clouds and close view. Compare the endpoint on Jupiter.]

Choose Earth and descend slowly from space. First comes the whole blue globe, then white clouds, and finally dunes or ice at the selected site. Pause and look: clouds move while land stays beneath them. Now try Jupiter. Will you find ground to stand on? Its bands and swirls are atmosphere, which becomes denser farther down.

---

03 · The Sun changes too
[Play and pause solar activity. Identify granules, spots and prominences, then inspect the fusion core.]

Return to the Sun. It shines with energy released by fusion deep inside. Look for small bright and dark granules and darker sunspots. Sunspots are cooler than their surroundings, but still extremely hot. Arches at the edge represent prominences, where magnetic fields support solar material. Pause to inspect them. There is no solid ground here.

---

04 · Companions around planets
[Compare Earth’s and Saturn’s moon systems, enter Titan, then use the parent button to return.]

Earth has the Moon, and Saturn has many moons too. They orbit their planets and travel with them around the Sun. Choose Titan and approach its hazy world. Its lakes contain mainly methane and ethane, not water. Return to Saturn afterward. The close world and the distant solar system belong to the same connected place.
