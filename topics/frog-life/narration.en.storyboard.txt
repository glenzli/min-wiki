01 · More than getting bigger
[Visit both larval observations, then return to the frog.]

Begin with eggs in a pond. A tadpole hatches with a long tail and looks unlike a frog on the bank. On a leaf, a butterfly egg hatches into a caterpillar, not a miniature winged adult. Follow two different animals, each with its own progress, and look for new abilities as their bodies change.

---

02 · New legs, a remaining tail
[Pause with four limbs and a tail, then inspect gradual tail resorption.]

Move the frog's progress slowly. Find the hind legs, then the forelegs. Pause: four limbs and a tail can exist together! The tail is gradually resorbed and some material reused, rather than falling into the water. The froglet at the end is not yet mature; it still has growing to do.

---

03 · A pupa is not a long sleep
[Reveal the pupa diagram, then play and pause independent wing preparation.]

Select the butterfly's pupa and open the inside diagram. A quiet outside hides continuing development, supported by reserves accumulated as a caterpillar. Select the adult and watch its wings: small and crumpled, then expanded, then gradually hardening. Our playback reveals steps, not real development compressed into an actual few seconds.

---

04 · One question, different answers
[Switch the four comparison lenses and inspect the grasshopper no-pupa route diagram.]

Place the two animals side by side and compare food, breathing or movement. They did not start at the same time or grow at the same rate. Look at the grasshopper route too: egg, nymph, adult, with no pupa in between. Animals have different ways of growing. Watch wild animals in place and avoid touching delicate pupae and wings.
