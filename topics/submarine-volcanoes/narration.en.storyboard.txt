01 · Look beneath the sea
[Run deep water from zero to about 25%. Point to the vehicle lamp, then choose the vent close-up and compare solid skins, advancing seams and the few illustrative cutaways.]

Look, a mountain is hiding under the sea. Down here the vent has no sunlight, so a little lamp on the underwater vehicle helps us see. Magma that comes out is called lava. Seawater cools the outside quickly, while the inside may stay hot. Press Run and move closer. Can you find the dark skin and tiny glowing seams?

---

02 · One lobe after another
[Keep the close-up and run from 25% to the end. Compare new growth, crust formation and the fading orange interior.]

Do those rounded shapes look like pillows? They are not soft, and we must never touch them. Hot lava inside can keep pushing out, making another lobe. When the inside cools too, it becomes rock. Lava under the sea can have many shapes. This is just one example.

---

03 · Water meets magma
[Run shallow water to about 40%. Point separately to suspended underwater particles and white mist above the sea, then move to the endpoint to see deposited fragments.]

Now look at the example near the surface. When water meets very hot magma, violent changes can sometimes scatter fragments. Tiny particles make water cloudy. The white mist above the sea is different: it contains many condensed droplets. Depth alone cannot tell us whether an explosion will happen, and seawater does not put out every volcano.

---

04 · A little island, a big mountain
[Move through the island case and point to the four-stage strip and colored deposit bands. Near 75%, compare the two supplies and choose the section. Restore continued supply, move to 100%, and compare the dashed former outline.]

Now we squeeze a long story into a short scene. Rounded lava lobes first overlap into a deep base. Broader flows then spread into low places. Near the surface, some fragments fall back onto the slope and later lava can cover them. Try Early stop: the seamount remains, but it never reaches the air. With continued supply, the visible island is still only a small part of the mountain. Waves reshape it at the end.
