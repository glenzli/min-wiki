01 · Meet a tool
[Show the three uses and the prewritten-example label.]

Some computer tools can reply, suggest ideas, draw or identify things in pictures. AI is a name for this category of human-made computer systems. Different tools have different abilities. As with other tools, start with your task and consider whether the tool fits it.

---

02 · Separate the roles
[Select person, program, AI and robot while retaining their distinct pictures.]

A program gives instructions for a machine to execute. AI is built with programs too. A robot has a mechanical body that acts in the physical world, and need not use AI. People live, observe and form relationships. A person still needs to judge a tool’s suggestions.

---

03 · Look for evidence
[Explore side-counting, the material claim and the school-notice example.]

An answer that sounds true can still be wrong. You can count a triangle’s sides, check suitable observations and sources for materials, and read official notices for school hours. An answer may be correct, incorrect or missing information. Asking again is not the same as checking evidence.

---

04 · Use it thoughtfully
[Return to task cards and the request, checking and privacy reminders.]

When you want ideas, state your task clearly, then choose and edit the suggestions. Check factual information and keep private details private. Learn about tools with a trusted adult. Let a tool help while keeping your own observation and judgment.
