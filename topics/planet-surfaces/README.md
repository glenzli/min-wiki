# 行星的表面是什么样的 / Planetary surfaces

Eight comparative worlds: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Neptune, **Titan (a moon of Saturn)** and **55 Cancri e (a rocky exoplanet)**. Chinese/English and child/science reading modes. Cached procedural landscapes distinguish crater rims, Venusian volcanic relief, terrestrial coasts, Martian layered mesas, giant-planet cloud bands, Titan’s hazy lakeshores, and an inferred molten dayside. Globe, close-view, and section controls use finite transitions; slow observation pans or globe rotation run only when requested. Descent takes 20 seconds and retains its state when changing view.

`model.ts` owns material categories, evidence status, material encounters, and continuous descent-marker positions. `content.ts` owns explanations, visible evidence labels, and source links. `surfacePainter.ts` owns deterministic slope-lit terrain, shoreline-constrained reflections and globe texture preparation. `surfaceWorker.ts` executes that expensive raster preparation away from UI events and transfers owned RGBA pixel buffers; the page copies them into its own canvas instead of relying on cross-worker ImageBitmap rendering, which was blank in the in-app WebKit browser. `scene.ts` owns the single active job, eight-world cache, obsolete-job termination, stale-result rejection, globe lighting and uncluttered sections. Worker failures settle into a small bounded illustrative silhouette and the readable explanation; they never retry automatically. Disposal terminates jobs and releases cached canvas backing stores. Empty or malformed pixel results are rejected and use the visible fallback. All labels and evidence captions sit outside the canvas. The canvas has no scientific labels to overlap the material boundaries or descent path.

The worlds deliberately distinguish **water**, **hydrocarbons**, **silicate melt**, and **dense giant-planet fluids**. Titan is not a water-ocean planet: observed methane–ethane lakes mostly occur in polar regions, on an icy moon. Its close landscape is a representative polar shore, not the Huygens landing site. An icy lakebed is an illustrative composition model, not a measurement of all beds. The magma ocean of 55 Cancri e is inferred, not photographed: extent and depth remain unknown, so the section has no asserted ocean floor. Neptune is an ice giant, not a demonstrated exposed-ocean planet, and neither giant provides a landing surface.

Textures are explanatory illustrations, not measured maps. Displayed globe sizes, relief, color, layer thickness, illumination, and descent distances are uncalibrated. The descent marker does not imply spacecraft survivability. `learning.json` includes bilingual academic explanations, evidence boundaries, and four child-friendly narration segments.

Primary sources checked 2026-09-18:

- [NASA Mercury facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/facts/) — craters, smooth plains, and grey-brown rock.
- [NASA Venus facts](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/), [Earth](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/), [Mars](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/), [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/).
- [NASA Neptune facts](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/neptune-facts/) — no solid surface; enhanced Voyager images exaggerate blue.
- [NASA Titan facts](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/titan/facts/) and [Cassini Titan science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/science/titan/) — hydrocarbon lakes, ice, atmosphere, and evidence.
- [NASA Webb: 55 Cancri e](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-hints-at-possible-atmosphere-surrounding-rocky-exoplanet/) and [Hu et al., Nature (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07432-x) — thermal and spectral evidence, likely molten surface, secondary atmosphere interpretation.
