01 · A green body in a dry place
[Show the storage view and the whole cactus.]

Some cacti stay green through long dry periods. Does that mean they need no water? No. Many living cells in their fleshy stems store water, rather like distributing reserves among many small spaces. The stem is neither an empty bottle nor an endless supply.

---

02 · After the rain
[Play rain, pause at the roots, then enlarge storage cells. In the surface view, follow brown carbon-dioxide markers inward and blue vapour markers outward.]

Make rain in the picture. Water taken up by roots gradually fills out the stem. Then let dry time pass. Pause and compare the stem and water store: are they shrinking? A cactus uses its reserves and eventually needs replenishment. Our buttons fast-forward a much slower real process. Enlarge the tissue and notice blue filling inside many cells. Rain has to reach the roots before storage rises; pressing a button does not fill a hollow bottle. Pause or scrub backward to inspect each part.

---

03 · Tiny doors by day and night
[Compare daytime and nighttime pores in the skin view.]

Look at the pores called stomata. Many desert cacti take in carbon dioxide mainly during cooler nights and keep pores more closed by day to save water. They initially store the carbon dioxide for later use. Life does not stop in daytime; different parts of the work are scheduled differently.

---

04 · Several features work together
[Return to the whole plant and indicate ribs, skin and spines.]

A waxy surface helps retain water, while spines and body shape also affect exposure. Several features work together, and cactus species differ; not all live in hot deserts. When you next see one, inspect its ribs and green stem carefully without touching the sharp spines.
