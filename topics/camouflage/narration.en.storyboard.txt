01 · Who is hiding in the forest?
[Keep the forest and guide attention to branches, leaves and bark.]

Three insects are hiding here. There is no race; take your time. One resembles a twig, another a leaf, and another blends with bark. Follow the branches with your eyes, then inspect the leaf edges. Can you spot an extra leg or a slender antenna?

---

02 · Change only the background
[Move the background slider gradually, then repeat the comparison from one close-up view.]

Pause and remember the positions. Keep every animal unchanged and replace the forest with a plain background. Are they easier to see? Their bodies have not changed, only their surroundings. A brown pebble can likewise be harder to notice on soil than on white paper. You can remove the background gradually instead of switching all at once. After finding an outline, inspect its fine legs and patterns up close, then return to the whole scene. The view is enlarged, not the insect’s body.

---

03 · Colour and shape work together
[Select all three insects without changing their appearance.]

Select each insect to inspect its strategy. The stick insect has a twig-like body; the leaf insect has leaf-like edges and patterns; the moth’s markings fit the bark. Sometimes a hunter misses an animal; sometimes it sees it but mistakes it for a leaf. The insect remains an animal.

---

04 · Look again with patience
[Restore the forest, reset markers and observe again.]

Restore the forest and look again. It may be easier because you remember the hiding places, not because the camouflage has changed. Different observers and lighting also affect the result. On a walk, examine shapes and colours quietly; some small animals reward patient attention.
