01 · Look at the whole Earth
[Approach slowly from the globe while following the same yellow square.]

We live on a round Earth. From far away, air hugs its edge. Remember the yellow marker: we will keep watching the same surface site.

---

02 · Approach winds and clouds
[Pause at the surface and troposphere; identify airflow arrows and clouds and rain.]

Now the ground and low clouds become clear. Wind carries air around. Warm, moist air can rise, cool and form clouds. Droplets or ice must grow before rain or snow can fall.

---

03 · Keep looking outward
[Follow the same radial path through the stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere and exosphere; locate the surface square below.]

Higher layers do different things. Ozone absorbs some ultraviolet light. Farther up, meteors can leave luminous streaks and polar regions can show auroras. Eventually the air becomes extremely sparse and blends into space.

---

04 · Put another sky beside ours
[Compare Venus, Mars, Titan and the near-vacuum cases. Explain that aligned rows do not mean equal heights.]

Keep Earth on the left and choose another world on the right. Its clouds may be different, or it may be almost airless. The Moon and Mercury have no Earth-like weather, but a few gas particles remain.
