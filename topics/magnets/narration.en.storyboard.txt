01 · It moves before touching
[Select iron, approach slowly and pause when movement begins.]

Watch the paper clip. As the magnet comes closer, the clip can start moving before contact, almost as if an invisible hand were pulling it. The magnet’s field is acting on it. Approach slowly instead of pushing all the way at once, and find the part where movement becomes noticeable.

---

02 · Try other materials
[Select copper, aluminum and wood, pausing for two seconds each.]

Try copper, aluminum and wood with the same procedure. Predict first and give yourself time to notice the result. Copper and aluminum are metals, but do not visibly follow this ordinary magnet like the iron paper clip. Knowing that something is a metal does not settle how strongly a magnet attracts it.

---

03 · Meet another magnet
[Choose magnet and compare before and after flipping, pausing three seconds.]

Bring in another magnet. Approach and see whether they join, then flip one. Pause: do they come together or move apart now? Changing the facing poles changes the result. This tray prevents unrestricted turning so we can see the difference between attraction and repulsion clearly.

---

04 · Remember how to compare
[Return to ready state and indicate material and flip controls.]

The useful skill is changing one condition at a time: first material, then orientation. That helps us notice causes. Never put small magnets in your mouth or remove them from toys. Ask an adult to provide a suitable large magnet, explain your predictions and discoveries, and think of your next question together.
