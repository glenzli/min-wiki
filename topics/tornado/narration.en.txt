A funnel cloud can look like a long finger reaching from a cloud. Today we will look beyond the outline and ask what the air is doing. A tornado contains violently rotating air connecting cloud and ground. We can investigate safely on screen, without approaching a real storm.

In some strong thunderstorms, winds differ with height while rising air tilts and stretches rotation. Several conditions must work together for a tornado to develop. Not every rotating thunderstorm becomes one. Change the updraft slowly and observe how the organized rotation changes.

Try a special comparison: hide the white condensation cloud. Pause. Are the rotation clues still there? The cloud makes some air movement visible, but no visible cloud does not mean no rotating air. Ordinary wind can be invisible too; moving leaves help us notice that it is passing.

A wide or narrow funnel, and a dark or pale color, cannot directly tell us the wind speed. Scientists combine several kinds of observations. Separating visible cloud from moving air is a useful discovery. If there is a tornado warning, go with an adult to the advised shelter rather than a window to search for a funnel.
