A fossil may preserve part of a body, a footprint, scratches or a chemical signal. By combining these clues with rocks, dates and environments, we can gradually reconstruct an ancient world of producers, consumers, decomposers and habitats.

This hall has three workbenches: [communities and evidence](#communities-en) → [filters in the fossil record](#fossils-en) → [Carboniferous plant structures](#plants-en). The seven communities compare specified places and intervals. They are not seven compulsory stages of evolutionary improvement. Choose one scene, predict a relationship, then examine its evidence.

## Workbench one: investigate the evidence behind a community {#communities}

### Begin with a distinction: an ecological web is not a family tree

Animals eating plants, microbes decomposing remains and corals building habitat are ecological relationships. Evolutionary trees instead concern common ancestry and branching. The questions connect, but providing someone's food does not make an organism that consumer's ancestor. Arrows here may also show habitat, material cycling or environmental pressure: read their labels.

Eukaryote, multicellular organism and animal are also different terms. Eukaryote describes cellular organization characterized by a nucleus and other structures; multicellular describes a body made from many cells. Animals occupy only part of the evolutionary tree: multicellular plants and fungi are not animals. The age of a particular multicellular algal fossil is not the common birthday of all eukaryotes or animals. [Research dating Bangiomorpha](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/7brz9-8mw86)

### What kinds of evidence can you inspect for the same object?

Choose a focal object and switch between reconstruction and four evidence views. These categories are not a fixed ranking of strength. Strong explanations often combine independent constraints.

| Evidence view | What it can contribute | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | Shells, bones, teeth, impressions, appendages, leaves and internal structures | One outline need not establish color, all soft tissues or diet |
| Trace | Tracks, burrows, scratches and changes to sediment made by activity | Every trace need not have one uniquely identifiable maker |
| Chemistry | Organic molecules, isotopes and mineral reactions constrain life and environments | Origin, preservation and later alteration matter; a signal is usually not a species list |
| Comparison and inference | Functional anatomy, living analogues, relationships and models help explain ways of life | Interpretations need assumptions and can change with new specimens or experiments |
| Reconstruction | Combines constraints into bodies, poses and environments that can be examined | Colors, spacing and some structures without specimen-level support are authored completion |

Fossils may preserve bodies or the traces of activity, and preservation can change their materials. Colors in this hall are display choices without individual pigment-specimen evidence. This does not mean ancient colors are forever beyond scientific investigation. [NPS: different ways fossils form](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/fossils/how-fossils-form.htm)

### Where and when are the seven communities?

| Representative community | Place and approximate interval used here | First relationship to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microbial shore | A representative shallow shore around 2.7 billion years ago, compared mechanistically with oxygenation around 2.4-2.1 billion years ago | Organic production, decomposition and oxygen-consuming reactions |
| Ediacaran matground | White Sea region, around 558 million years ago | Large soft bodies using microbial mats and leaving activity traces |
| Burgess Shale sea | Representative Canadian fossil assemblage, around 508 million years ago | Swimming predators, bottom dwellers, sponges and food resources |
| Coal forest | Equatorial Euramerican wetlands around 310 million years ago | Carbon passing among plants, decomposers, peat and burial |
| End-Permian shallow sea | A representative Tethyan web before the crisis around 252 million years ago, compared with Early Triassic assemblages | Lost reef structure, suspension feeders and surviving branches |
| End-Cretaceous land | North American forest-river mosaics around 66 million years ago and representative early Paleocene assemblages | Resource pathways and surviving branches after disrupted primary production |
| Mammoth steppe | A northern Eurasian and Beringian regional mosaic around 40,000-12,000 years ago | Herbs, shrubs, large herbivores and soil feedbacks |

These dates concern **particular records or chosen representative windows**, rather than the beginnings of whole geological periods. The Burgess Shale is a later preservation window within the Cambrian, not the start of the Cambrian or all animals. The scenes do not combine Chengjiang, Burgess, White Sea and coal-swamp organisms into one supposed place and time.

### Can a world without animals or forests have an ecosystem?

Yes. Microbes can produce organic matter, use and decompose it, and change surrounding chemistry. Mat communities can also trap sediment and stabilize surfaces, leaving structures associated with biological activity. Physical processes can produce layering too, however. Researchers combine microstructure, sedimentary setting and chemistry rather than identifying a modern cyanobacterium from one stripe. [Noffke and colleagues: ancient and modern microbial mats](https://www.geosociety.org/gsatoday/archive/18/10/pdf/i1052-5173-18-10-4.pdf)

For example, the diversity and environmental associations of the roughly 3.43-billion-year-old Strelley Pool structures in Australia support biological interpretations of stromatolites. They do not directly identify modern microbial species or prove that this community already produced oxygen. Our approximately 2.7-billion-year-old functional illustration is likewise not a complete cell inventory from one specimen. [Allwood and colleagues, 2006](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04764)

The origins of photosynthesis, oxygen-producing metabolism and sustained atmospheric oxygen accumulation are connected but different questions. Respiration and reactions with reduced material consume oxygen, while changing environments alter opportunities for life. Anaerobes can remain in low-oxygen pores and other habitats. The Great Oxidation did not poison every microbe at once, nor does an oxygen increase automatically generate animals. Sulfur isotopes constrain ancient atmospheric change without independently identifying an oxygen-producing cell. [Farquhar and colleagues, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10926533/), [Smithsonian early-life explanation](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/life-science/early-life-earth-animal-origins)

**Explore together:** Keep organic matter as your focus and follow production, consumption and burial. In the oxygenation comparison, distinguish environmental changes from functional groups. Explain why it is not a continuous movie of one sediment surface across hundreds of millions of years.

### Can White Sea bodies and scratches reveal a meal?

Dickinsonia preserves oval impressions with repeated units. A common name is not evidence that it was a modern jellyfish. Sterols in particular organically preserved specimens support an animal affinity, without establishing all its organs, exact feeding movements or the relationships of every Ediacaran organism. [Bobrovskiy and colleagues, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat7228)

Kimberella adds body impressions, associated scratches and molecular evidence. These support interpretations of movement and feeding on the bottom; describing it as mollusc-like still requires qualification rather than substitution with a modern snail. Repeated impressions and fan-shaped scratch sets can constrain movement and mat use, but are not preserved motion pictures. [White Sea fossils and food molecules](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2022/november/evidence-worlds-oldest-meal-may-have-been-discovered.html), [research on Ediacaran locomotion traces](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/9/9/395)

**Explore together:** Inspect the body first, then traces. Separate the existence of an impression, an interpretation of how it formed, and the completion of a living pose. What correspondence between a trace and body would make an explanation stronger?

### Why not let Anomalocaris crush any trilobite?

Fossils constrain the eyes, frontal appendages, lateral swimming flaps and tail fan of Anomalocaris canadensis. Appendage movement and biomechanics better support agile capture of soft prey. Wounds on trilobites or fossil feces containing shell fragments do not identify Anomalocaris as the attacker by themselves. Our soft-prey node represents a functional group, rather than one worm assigned as a proven meal. [ROM Anomalocaris specimens and interpretations](https://burgess-shale.rom.on.ca/fossils/anomalocaris-canadensis/), [Bicknell and colleagues, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37403497/)

The dorsal exoskeleton of Olenoides serratus has a central axis and two lateral lobes: trilobites did not have only three segments. Some specimens also preserve underside appendages, informing questions about movement and food handling on the bottom. Sponges are animals; bodies and skeletal elements help investigate suspension feeding. Remaining attached to the seafloor does not make them plants. [ROM: Olenoides serratus](https://burgess-shale.rom.on.ca/fossils/olenoides-serratus/)

This fossil deposit also reveals a recording effect. Exceptional preservation retains appendages and soft structures usually lost to decay. Seeing more unusual animals in one layer may reflect both a real community and a special preservation window; it need not mean all differences resulted from suddenly faster evolution. [ROM Burgess Shale research](https://burgess-shale.rom.on.ca/)

**Explore together:** Pick a food-to-consumer link. Is it supported by food found inside a body, functional anatomy, or a broad functional illustration? Explain separately why it is not an ancestry arrow.

### Why is a coal forest not an enlarged modern woodland?

The representative wetland around 310 million years ago combines channels, peat swamps and higher ground, rather than all Carboniferous continents. Arborescent lycopsids, calamites and seed ferns could form tall vegetation with different structures and reproductive backgrounds. Tree describes a growth form, not one lineage leading from an ancient trunk to every modern tree. Smaller land plants and early rooting systems preceded this forest: forests were not the first colonization of land. [Kenrick and Strullu-Derrien, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25187527/)

Photosynthesis transfers carbon from carbon dioxide into organic matter. Some returns through respiration and decomposition; some remains under suitable water levels, depositional space and sustained burial. Peat still needs compaction and thermal alteration to become coal. More trees alone do not guarantee coal formation. Decomposition existed in the Carboniferous, and a supposed absence of lignin-degrading fungi does not explain the whole coal-production peak. [Nelsen and colleagues, 2016](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/28188)

**Explore together:** Follow two destinations from a plant: decomposers or long-term burial. Which requires additional environmental conditions? Then examine plant structures at the workshop without treating the forest's colors as measured originals.

### Does a surviving broad group mean there were no losses?

No. End-Permian seas lost trilobites and branches including rugose and tabulate corals. Brachiopods, bivalves and other broad groups retained surviving lineages, yet their composition and diversity changed profoundly. Keeping a bivalve identity across the comparison anchors a broad group. It does not establish the same species, survival of every member, or immunity of bivalves generally. [NPS: different extinction events and their impacts](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/fossils/mass-extinctions-through-geologic-time.htm)

Large-scale volcanism and carbon-cycle disruption can impose several environmental pressures. Warming can increase animal oxygen demand alongside changes in marine oxygen supply. Acidification, hypoxia and food-web disruption require regional and taxonomic distinctions. Physiological models can be checked against fossil distributions without explaining every death through one cause. Some restructuring began before the extinction boundary; recovery is not one fixed progress bar. [Penn and colleagues, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30523082/), [Clapham and Bottjer, 2007](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0705280104)

The end-Cretaceous crisis had a different explanation. Geological evidence for a large asteroid impact around 66 million years ago is strong. Dust and other consequences affected light and primary production, spreading effects through food webs. Discussing background volcanism does not give it equal evidential weight with the impact for this event. [Smithsonian impact and fossil evidence](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/paleontology/mass-extinction-solving-dinosaur-mystery), [Hull and colleagues, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31949074/)

Birds are a branch within dinosaurs. Many birds also disappeared near the boundary while some lineages continued. Mammals already existed in the Cretaceous and suffered losses too. Research relating forest collapse, ground-dwelling habits and resources proposes constrained explanations of survival selectivity, rather than a guaranteed formula for every bird or small mammal. [Field and colleagues, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29804807/)

| What is compared? | Different possible answers |
| --- | --- |
| Diversity | The number of groups or species; numerical comparisons need the same denominator and sampling conditions |
| Ecological roles | Whether filtering, feeding, predation or habitat construction persists |
| Community composition | Which particular members occupy those roles, and whether they match the earlier community |
| Recovery | Places and indicators can recover differently; a returning role does not resurrect an extinct species |

**Explore together:** Find a link broken by the loss of reef builders in the end-Permian comparison, then inspect damaged primary-producer pathways at the end-Cretaceous. Explain what ended, was reduced, or continued. Early Triassic and early Paleocene assemblages are not reports from the day after disaster.

### Was mammoth steppe empty tundra beside an ice sheet?

Lower sea levels connected Asia and North America through Beringia, much of which was not ice-covered. Cold, relatively dry Late Pleistocene conditions supported regional mosaics of herbs, shrubs, wetlands and large animals, rather than an enlarged copy of modern tundra. Mammoths, horses and bison need not share one diet. Feeding, trampling and nutrient return influence the ground in turn. [NPS: Beringian megafauna and ecological mosaics](https://www.nps.gov/articles/aps-17-1-4.htm)

Bones, teeth, frozen remains, plant macrofossils, pollen and sedimentary DNA each have preservation and sampling biases. Climate, vegetation, people and their interactions require regional evidence. Last records differ across populations and places. Our 40,000-12,000-year scene window is not a universal extinction interval for all mammoths and does not imply that all horses went globally extinct. [MacDonald and colleagues, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22692536/)

**Explore together:** Choose one animal-to-vegetation feedback and one environmental influence on plants. Suggest evidence for each, then explain why one record cannot eliminate all other causes.

## Workbench two: why do the same 12 remains produce different fossil records? {#fossils}

### What happens between being alive and being discovered?

Material from life or death passes through several filters. Does it reach this deposit? What survives decay, scavenging and abrasion? Is it buried and preserved? Does the rock survive? Where and how do we search? Rapid burial and more durable hard parts often favor preservation; exceptional conditions can preserve soft tissues. Fossils can also be impressions, casts, mineral infillings or replacements, rather than whole bodies simply turning to stone. [NPS taphonomy](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/fossils/taphonomy.htm)

The experiment fixes two examples each of shells, worm-like soft bodies, leaves, wood, bones and insects: 12 identities in total. The adjacent land-and-sea source collection stays the same. Selecting marine, river or peat deposition changes which remains reach that setting. Burial conditions change survival and destruction; search extent changes only which preserved remains are collected.

| Observed destination | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- |
| Outside this deposit's source record | Source and transport conditions did not deliver it here; it need not have been absent nearby |
| Destroyed | Not preserved as an identifiable remain under these rules; wider sampling cannot restore it |
| Preserved but uncollected | Material remains, but this search has not found it |
| Found | Included in this collection, without automatically giving its original abundance |

The sequence starts paused at the source. Explicitly play the four-station illustration over about nine seconds, pause/reset it, or scrub directly to 100 for the complete destinations. Reduced motion changes only between stations. Seconds compress the explanatory order, not fossilization time; scrubbing neither restores destroyed material nor revives extinct organisms.

### Can the proportion collected reveal how many animals originally lived there?

Not in this experiment. It uses deterministic teaching filters without calibrated natural probabilities or ecological counts. For example, at the endpoint marine deposition with rapid burial gives three finds in the small search and eight in the wide search. Four source-excluded objects still do not appear. Keeping wide sampling but switching to exposure leaves only two finds. These numbers concern **the destinations of these 12 objects under the stated rules**, rather than a natural soft-body preservation rate or extinction percentage.

Searching more widely reduces some sampling gaps without independently repairing preservation bias. Similarly, the last recovered fossil may predate the last living individual. A gap may reflect missing deposition, later erosion or absent sampling. Origin and extinction judgments need chronology, stratigraphy and several kinds of record together.

**Explore together:** First play to the endpoint or scrub to 100. Hold source and burial fixed and widen the search. Which identities change destination? Keep the wide search but change burial. Which gaps remain impossible to fill? Use one particular identity to distinguish not finding it from its never existing.

## Workbench three: bring a coal-forest structure closer {#plants}

### Where should you look on three different tree-like plants?

| Representative structure | First inspect a constrained feature | What reconstruction still adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lepidodendron-type lycopsid | Diamond-shaped leaf cushions and smaller scars marking former leaf attachment | Overall crown, colors and leaf density; cushions are not reptile scales |
| Calamites-type arborescent horsetail relative | Nodes, internodes and associated branch and leaf-whorl evidence | A qualified external tree form; ribs on common pith casts are not automatically complete outer bark |
| Medullosan-type seed-fern frond | Main rachis, pinnae, pinnules and branching veins | Three-dimensional opening and some counts; reproduction requires separate seeds or reproductive-organ evidence |

Stem-surface fossils constrain cushion patterns, calamite casts constrain part of internal and external architecture, and frond impressions can preserve venation. All three models are procedural constructions, not scans of complete living plants or tools that identify unknown specimens. Seeds have not been attached at unverified positions. [University of Michigan plant specimens](https://umorf.ummp.lsa.umich.edu/wp/mi-backyard-fossils-plants/), [Kentucky Geological Survey Calamites guide](https://www.uky.edu/KGS/fossils/fossil-month-12-2021-calamites.php)

### Why might a fern-like leaf not belong to a fern?

Similar shapes can occur in different lineages. Seed fern is a morphological term for several extinct seed-plant groups, rather than one simple natural branch. They had fern-like leaves but reproduced with seeds. A leaf alone cannot establish the reproductive system of the whole plant. The NPS specimen comes from the later Permian and illustrates this distinction; it is not the same specimen placed into our approximately 310-million-year-old wetland. [NPS seed-fern specimen](https://www.nps.gov/articles/seed-fern.htm)

### How can separate leaves, trunks and roots become one reconstructed plant?

Ancient plant organs often fossilize separately and can receive separate names. Reliable assembly requires evidence such as attachment, anatomical continuity, matching structure and stratigraphic context. Coming from similar layers or looking as though they belong together is usually insufficient. Living relatives inform comparisons, but enlarging a modern horsetail does not reproduce Calamites, and a lycopsid is not a palm.

**Explore together:** Rotate a plant and identify one directly fossil-constrained structure, one whole-plant inference and one display completion. In surface detail, can you explain whether the clue represents an exterior, an internal cast or a leaf impression?

## Fourth workbench: Waptia—from flattened clues to a body in space {#waptia}

About 508 million years ago, Waptia was a mandibulate arthropod in the Burgess ecosystem. Resemblance to a shrimp does not make it a modern shrimp. This workbench presents a complete body and returns to real fossils to check it. Its setting is the Walcott Quarry context; the research plate contains different Burgess specimens and does not assign every photograph to that exact quarry.

Start with the two valves of the anterior shield, then follow the different appendages. There is one pair each of antennules, mandibles and maxillules, four post-maxillular pairs, then six long-lamellate pairs: thirteen in total, with eyes counted separately. Six lamellate pairs correspond to only five visible middle segments because the last two somites fuse. The posterior abdomen has six limbless somites, followed by paired caudal rami, each with three pseudosegments. These details chiefly follow the [2018 redescription](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6030330/), rather than counting structures in attractive older artwork.

The representative model has a 50 mm midline body length, measured from the anterior median sclerite to the caudal-ramus tip, excluding antennules. Most of 28 complete specimens measured in the paper were about 40–60 mm; that research sample is not a living-population census. Perspective foreshortens the screen projection, and fine setae are slightly thickened. Flattened fossil widths and wrinkles do not directly determine living thickness.

Select the same model's head, shield, limbs or tail to compare fossil constraints, volumetric completion and remaining unknowns. The real plate retains its original 1 cm scale bars. Panel 1b is USNM 139214; the panels are different individuals. The [original figure and caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6030330/figure/RSOS172206F1/) remain independently checkable. You can also [download the gallery's unmodified plate](assets/waptia-fossils-fig1.jpg?download=1), with attribution and the CC BY 4.0 license beside the image and in the asset record.

Play a finite ten-second example of one possible coordinated lamellar stroke. Pause, scrub, reset or compare neutral morphology. Short anterior limbs are not treated as walking legs, and no prey capture is staged. Stroke frequency, color, living fullness and lighting include authored completion. No actual swimming speed, respiratory rate or whole-model confidence percentage is provided. Fading the shield helps inspect obscured structures; it does not imply physiological transparency. If 3D fails, part explanations, the real fossil plate and the original research remain available.

## Deeper: a period's name is not an evidence age {#chronology}

A stratigraphic boundary, a fossil deposit's age and an event's duration are different quantities. This table gives selected period bases relevant to the vocabulary used here, following the [international chart, version 2026/06](https://stratigraphy.org/ICSchart/ChronostratChart2026-06.pdf), including its quoted uncertainties. These are not the individual uncertainties of every specimen or community above.

| Base of geological period | Age (Ma, million years before present) | How it relates to the scenes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cambrian | 538.8 ± 0.6 | The roughly 508 Ma Burgess Shale is younger than the start of the Cambrian; Cambrian diversification was not instantaneous |
| Ordovician | 486.85 ± 1.5 | A stratigraphic boundary, not the shared birthday of every marine animal |
| Silurian | 443.1 ± 0.9 | Boundary ages, a glacier's maximum extent and particular extinction pulses require separate investigation |
| Devonian | 419.62 ± 1.36 | Our coal forest is not the beginning of earlier plant colonization or root evolution |
| Carboniferous | 358.86 ± 0.19 | The roughly 310 Ma wetland lies within this period, rather than representing all its environments |

The earliest fossil found provides a current constraint, not automatically an instantaneous origin for a lineage. Even the oldest boundary on a geological chart is not Earth's exact birthday stopwatch. Formation and evolution can be extended processes.

## Ecological webs and evolutionary trees answer different questions

Ecological webs connect energy, habitat, material cycling and environmental pressure. Evolutionary trees concern common ancestry and branching. The seven scenes represent different places and times, not individuals turning into the next community. The representative shore around 2.7 billion years ago contains microbes, not land plants or animals; the timing and identity of early oxygen producers remain qualified. Before-and-after extinction labels retain broad-group anchors, without guaranteeing the same species or every member survived. Eukaryote, multicellular organism and animal refer to cellular organization, body construction and a lineage respectively; the earliest recovered record is not an instantaneous origin.

## The fossil record passes through several filters

Evidence must reach a depositional area, survive decay or breakage, be preserved through burial and later history, and be sampled. Bodies, tracks, scratches, organic molecules and isotopes answer different questions; absence from a collection is not direct proof of biological absence. The experiment retains 12 remains and uses deterministic teaching rules to compare source, preservation and sampling. Its counts are not natural abundance, fossilization probabilities or calibrated sampling rates. A wider search cannot restore material already destroyed. An explicitly started sequence compresses four observation stations into about nine seconds. Scrubbing revisits a teaching record rather than reversing nature or reviving organisms. Destinations are revealed only when the sequence reaches their stage.

## Plant architecture, connected organs and long-term carbon

Tree describes a growth form rather than a single lineage. Arborescent lycopsids, calamites and seed ferns differ in structure and reproductive background. Cushions, internodes, fronds and venation provide clues; connecting separate stems, leaves, roots and seeds into a whole plant requires attachment, anatomy and stratigraphic evidence. Some fixed carbon returns through decomposition; some remains under suitable water, accommodation and burial conditions. Carboniferous communities had decomposers, and peat is not already thermally mature coal. The complete Waptia 3D likewise combines several specimens: thirteen appendage pairs, a fused middle segment and six limbless abdominal somites have anatomical support, while living thickness, color and stroke rhythm include completion. The model uses a representative 50 mm midline length and does not treat one flattened photograph as a living-body scan.

## A common misconception

Reconstructions are not ancient photographs, and ecological arrows are not ancestry arrows. A common name does not make Dickinsonia a modern jellyfish; trilobites did not have only three segments; Anomalocaris is not automatically a machine for crushing adult trilobites. Birds are a dinosaur branch, mammals already existed in the Cretaceous, and survival does not mean every member escaped loss. Lycopsids are not palms, seed ferns are not modern true ferns, and missing fossils do not prove absence.

## Model limits

Communities combine representative organisms and functional groups from specified fossil records, regions and times. They are not complete species lists, abundance estimates or guarantees that every organism coexisted at one place. Microbial comparisons span broad intervals; extinction aftermath is not the next day. Evidence views distinguish records, inference and unknowns. Colors, some soft tissues, poses and spacing are original completion. The 12 remains and filtering thresholds are deterministic illustrations without calibrated probabilities, ages or natural preservation rates; nine seconds of playback is not a fossilization time. Plant 3D is procedural, not scanned; size, crowns, leaf counts and opening angles are not measurements or identification tools. Workbenches share no real ecological clock and simulate no actual population evolution. Waptia anatomy chiefly follows the 2018 redescription, at 10 mm per world unit. Its representative 50 mm midline body length runs from the anterior median sclerite to the caudal-ramus tip, excluding antennules. Setae are thickened; parts of volume, pose and color are completed. The original CC BY fossil plate retains scale bars beside the model; the two are not claimed as one individual. The ten-second lamellar stroke calibrates neither actual frequency nor speed and depicts no prey capture. Shield fading is an inspection treatment.

### No forests, but an interdependent world

Long ago, a shore without trees or animals could still contain an interdependent microbial world. Some microbes made organic matter; others used and decomposed it. Oxygen later accumulated and changed environments, yet low-oxygen habitats continued to support many microbes.

*Scene cue: Inspect functional links on the representative microbial shore, then compare the broad oxygenation interval. This is not one rock's continuous movie or the extinction of all anaerobes.*

### Look at bodies and relationships

A body impression, a set of scratches and a chemical signal provide different clues. Ancient seas held bottom dwellers, filter feeders and swimming predators. Environmental crises ended some branches while others continued. Continuing does not mean every original member survived. Waptia’s shield, eyestalks and different limbs can also be checked against fossils. Giving it volume and movement still requires a careful distinction between evidence and completion.

*Scene cue: Switch body and trace evidence in Ediacaran and Burgess scenes. Compare the end-Permian and end-Cretaceous aftermath, identifying food-web links rather than ancestry. Compare complete Waptia 3D with the original fossil plate. Distinguish thirteen appendage pairs from eyes and show finite lamellar motion, qualifying color, volume and rhythm as completion.*

### Which filters could hide the evidence?

A remain may never reach this deposit. It may decay or break before burial, or be preserved but never found. Searching more widely can reveal additional surviving clues, but cannot bring a destroyed body back.

*Scene cue: Play the four stations, then pause and retain all 12 identities while varying setting, burial and search extent separately. At the endpoint inspect source gaps, destruction, uncollected remains and finds. Scrubbing only revisits the illustrative record.*

### Approach a different kind of tree

Some ancient trees left diamond-shaped leaf cushions. Others connected branches at repeated nodes. Some had fern-like leaves but belonged to seed-producing plants. Decomposers worked in these forests too. Only some organic matter remained buried under suitable conditions and could later become coal.

*Scene cue: Rotate the three plant forms and identify cushions, nodes and fronds. Separate fossil constraints, comparisons and artistic completion without calling the procedural 3D a scan.*
