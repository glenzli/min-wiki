01 · Begin with the same start
[Play together, select a contact, scrub back and replay, then change initial speed.]

Three identical blocks start equally fast. No one keeps pushing after release; contact with the surface slows them. Select a lane. Teal motion energy shrinks while orange transferred energy grows, keeping the same total. Would a faster start travel farther?

---

02 · A rolling wheel and its contact
[Compare rolling and locked-wheel cases at the same displacement, then identify the disc and road contacts.]

Now follow the same gold mark on a wheel. At the bottom, it touches the road without sliding along it for that instant. A locked wheel can instead slide as a whole. A car has two different contacts: pads rub the brake disc, and tires rely on the road’s force to slow the whole vehicle.

---

03 · Reaction takes distance too
[Compare 30 versus 60 and dry versus wet separately; pause during reaction, braking and after stopping.]

A driver needs time to react. In the gold section, this braking action has not started yet. In the green section the car slows. Increase starting speed, then change the road, and compare both sections. These numbers are teaching choices, never a guide for deciding when to cross a real road.

---

04 · The body must slow too
[Inspect shoulder and lap belts, then rear-facing, forward-facing and booster seats; show no unsafe-fit or injury experiment. Compare 0, 50 and 100% in the snug-band loading window. Call the endpoint the first maximum relative displacement, not a completed vehicle stop.]

Forces slow the car, and real forces must slow our bodies too. A correctly fitted belt and suitable child seat help. The shoulder belt crosses shoulder and chest; the lap belt lies low over the hips. Never loosen the belt deliberately or hide it behind the back. Adults choose and install protection using the child’s size and both manuals, and secure every trip. The band is snug from the start. As the body advances relative to the car, the band stretches and pulls backward. This enlarged illustration explains the principle; it is no reason to leave belt slack.
