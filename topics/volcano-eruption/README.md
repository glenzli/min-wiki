# 火山：从岩浆到山、岛与湖 / Volcano explorer

One bilingual project at `/topics/volcano-eruption/` combines shield and composite volcanoes, an Omuroyama-inspired scoria cone, submarine volcanism and volcanic lakes. Four learning units contain seven question-led studies. Entry does not require naming a volcano: mechanism, eruption styles, retained mountain building, submarine growth, ecological conditions, lake retention, and groundwater heat. Landforms and environments are comparisons within the relevant question, not peer categories beside lake outcomes.

## Scientific scope

Mountain geometry, deposit timing, cooling appearance, weathering and vegetation are illustrative. There is no calibrated time axis or real-vent reconstruction. Omuro's eighteen teaching deposits and representative clasts do not count measured eruptions or establish conserved ejecta volume. Visible clast and soil/root scales aid inspection; they are not measured sizes. Shield volcanoes can erupt explosively; scoria cones can also produce lava. The default scoria landscape uses Omuroyama as an example, including the role of traditional burning in its current grassland. Its mountain shape formed during eruption, not as a consequence of extinction.

The 3D eruption chapter is a teaching reconstruction, not surveyed terrain, a subsurface scan or a reconstruction of a named historical eruption. Layer thickness, storage depth, sheet width and visible heat are chosen for legibility. The ash volume integrates a bounded procedural parcel-density field, without solving fluid dynamics, dispersal or hazard. A filling crater and its overflow are illustrative geometry, not a conserved-volume or calibrated lava model. The submarine chapter also has its own 3D reconstruction, retaining its distinct supply and accretion clock. All reachable case/chapter combinations now select their own three-dimensional projection; SVG/Canvas remain explicit fallbacks when WebGL is unavailable. The building question ends at retained deposition before ecological succession. The ecological question begins on an existing mountain, and the style comparison begins after the selected default pathways have connected. Their normalized progresses are not one calibrated volcanic lifecycle.

The material refinement distinguishes connected incandescent seams from rough crust rafts, with material-distance coordinates along the same terrain-bound flows and a rounded, low-contact flow front. The episode fixes crust, flow extent and cooling. `magmaMotion.ts` owns one bounded, separately pausable display phase: `meltTransport.ts` retains a thin moving melt surface within the existing lava ribbon; low-frequency folds and flow fronts travel along existing dike/flow coordinates. Fixed molten masks keep this transport inside the liquid region. Crystals, host rock and cold crust do not circulate with this detail. It updates at most 24 times per second while hot, visible and admitted; hiding, reduced motion, cooling and disposal stop work without catch-up. An explicit seek/replay resets the phase; ordinary draws and pausing process playback preserve the chosen flow setting. This is illustrative liquid identification, not measured circulation, velocity or a fluid solver.

The cutaway removes the front host rock while retaining the intrusive system's volumes, following the explanatory approach in [Moonraker's X-Ray Earth artwork](https://moonrakervfx.com/project/xray-earth/). Eleven irregular geological units have real depth, ordered contacts and natural pinching on the plain; they are illustrative subdivisions, not individual measured eruptions or the separate mountain-building clock's eighteen teaching deposits. Creased contact normals separate the rough exposed face from buried surfaces. A closed isosurface joins the original storage anchors into a crystal-bearing region, with fixed three-dimensional grain and interstitial heat detail, a partly exposed contact rind and local light. It does not represent a hollow cave or an open lava lake. Intrusions have two broad sides, finite thickness and closed tips that advance and retreat on the existing dike path. The deep feeder enters through the base. Widths and depths are deliberately visible and uncalibrated; there is no reconstructed tomography, measured melt fraction, invented circulation or temperature scale. No third-party artwork is bundled.

Ash uses a cached, periodic 64³ density texture (256 KiB of immutable CPU data), advected density erosion, and bounded light attenuation through that density. Each scene owns its GPU texture. A full-resolution scene color/depth target and a separate ash target (longest side at most 640 px; 48 ray steps on desktop and 36 on narrow screens) preserve terrain occlusion before compositing with one tone/color conversion. Non-ash frames use the original direct render path. Both render targets, their depth texture and the ash volume texture are disposed with the renderer. These bounds describe resource policy, not a measured frame-rate guarantee.

The lake question follows magma withdrawal, reduced support, fractures and sinking rock before water accumulation. It does not require a completely empty underground chamber or imply that every eruption triggers collapse.

Ecological recovery is a qualitative comparison, with compressed, uncalibrated time. Organisms need suitable substrate, water and temperatures; drought, cold, rapid drainage and renewed deposits can interrupt recovery. Neither a universal species sequence nor a forest endpoint is prescribed. Omuroyama’s managed grassland is distinguished from this general comparison.

The lake case is another basin history, not a claim that the selected land cone necessarily collapses or becomes a lake. Lake water must have input; a lake does not establish extinction. Dormancy and extinction assessments remain independent of shape, erosion, vegetation and eruption intensity. Deep/shallow/island submarine examples are selected conditions and timescales, not an inevitable sequence.

The mechanism is one recharge-driven scenario, with buoyancy and regional stress held fixed. Rock resistance is an illustrative aggregate barrier, not a measured strength. Pressure and connection bars are normalized conceptual indicators; there is no pressure-volume solver, calibrated fracture mechanics, or eruption forecast. Alternative tectonic triggers are described, not simulated. The lower feeder enters from greater depth: the shallow storage region is not the origin of melt and the drawing does not include the whole mantle. Cooling and crystallization are compressed teaching time.

The mechanism question opens with one underground episode in an already existing mountain, at the start of its own clock. Mountain building summarizes many retained deposits over a longer clock. These are not consecutive frames of one physically calibrated eruption. In the children view, mechanism outcomes compare a lava fountain, a lava flow, an ash-rich eruption, and an intrusion that stalls underground. Choosing a new mechanism outcome restarts that episode; the academic view retains continuous qualitative controls. Its explanation introduces net driving pressure Δp = p_m − σ_n and the fracture-mechanics roles of K_I and K_Ic as research entry points, while explicitly stating that neither is computed by the page. The separate eruption-style question compares connected default pathways without restarting its position. A compact disclosure groups the questions and reading modes; shapes and environments stay within the relevant question.

The integrated submarine chapter follows its selected vent with a continuous camera that expands as the edifice grows. A separate ocean panorama preserves the larger spatial context; section and vent close-ups remain available. The shield/composite landscape and ecology cameras frame the same three-dimensional mountain; the history section exposes four basement units and eighteen teaching deposits with shared contacts. A portrait history cutaway moves closer to the central intrusive system rather than shrinking the whole broad basement into the screen width. Omuro instead uses the same three-dimensional mountain for the full slope, crater close-up and rooted cutaway. Their continuous surrounding ground extends beyond the mountain, while cutaway lenses remove the front host rock. Camera size responds to the presentation stage's current aspect ratio. These are changes of teaching lens, not physical growth speed or measured surveys.

## Exposed lava skin and ejected melt

Surface-flow inspection distinguishes a cold, rough bank, transported cooling skin and small openings into a hotter interior. Bounded shear and folds move within the existing ribbon; local tearing exposes incandescent seams and overturned lips instead of illuminating the entire channel. The same retained surface coordinates carry skin and relief, and downstream/edge cooling increases dark cover. The original episode controls supply and final cooling; separately pausable display motion does not create a new source, advance a geological front or claim measured viscosity, temperature or crust-growth time. Underground crystal-bearing storage retains its own fixed framework and melt-mask boundary.

`ejecta3d.ts` retains the canonical finite fragment schedule and ground intersections. Rounded unequal droplets, spindles and short stretched clots depict hot ejected melt; a separate harder family remains for ash-rich fragments. Flight age darkens the rind, leaves bounded hot openings and reduces deformation. Still-plastic landing may flatten locally at the same impact site; cooled deposits retain their positions. Fountain skin follows the existing jet geometry and admitted display phase. No extra emission, trajectory, hidden material, particle lifetime or process clock is introduced. These appearances are uncalibrated reconstructions, without aerodynamic, fragmentation, thermal or rigid-body solvers.

## Eruption scenarios and relative strength

The surface-style question opens at a developed, connected episode position so terrain-bound lava is already visible. Six scene presets expose single-vent overflow, multiple discrete vents, low spatter, a high fountain, a low ash column and a stronger ash column. The paired small/large controls keep the existing mountain, process progress and camera. Gas and viscosity still determine the eruption form; strength changes relative source/product extent within that form. The fountain and ash pairs differ only in strength. Multiple vents divide the same source and do not automatically make the eruption larger. The three discrete vents do not claim a continuous surface fissure.

`eruptionScenarios.ts` owns the pure presets. `model.ts` supplies the canonical bounded strength weights; `eruptionGeometry.ts` and `canvasScene.ts` consume them for clast activity/trajectories, lava width/spread and ash height/radius/density. The 3D jet consumes the same weight. Legacy callers without a strength retain their prior default output. The labels are qualitative contrasts, not measured erupted volume, discharge, VEI classes or forecasts. The distinction between eruption size and rate follows [USGS: How big is an eruption?](https://www.usgs.gov/news/volcano-watch-how-big-eruption); compositional and eruption-style context follows [NPS: Volcanic eruptions](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/volcanoes/volcanic-eruptions.htm).
