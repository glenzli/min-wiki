01 · One half in sunlight
[Show sunlight arrows and the phase inset.]

The Moon sometimes looks round and sometimes curved. Imagine looking from space: sunlight arrives from one side and lights the half facing it. The Moon does not make its own light like the Sun. Its shape seems to change because sometimes we see more of that bright half and sometimes less.

---

02 · Follow it around
[Pause at new, quarter and full phases, allowing four seconds for the question. After reaching first quarter, enlarge the Earth view, then compare its maria at full Moon.]

Move slowly from new Moon to a half-lit disk and then full Moon. Pause and think: has the bright half disappeared, or has our viewing angle changed? Take your time, then enlarge the Earth view and find the dark markings. The light changes, but the surface has not been replaced. Both pictures show the same Moon from different places.

---

03 · It turns too
[Enable the surface marker and follow its Earth-facing direction.]

The Moon turns as it goes around Earth, keeping roughly the same side toward us. Imagine walking around a chair while always facing it. After one lap, you have turned all the way around too. The side facing away from Earth also receives sunlight. It is not always dark.

---

04 · Give distance its real scale
[Show the true-scale separation of about thirty Earth diameters.]

Now switch to true size and distance. There is a great deal of empty space between Earth and the Moon! Earlier we enlarged and brought the bodies closer to make phases easy to inspect. This view uses a different ruler. Remember that a crescent usually is not Earth’s shadow covering the Moon. An eclipse needs a special arrangement.
