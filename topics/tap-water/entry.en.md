Does clear water contain only water molecules, and does clarity prove it is safe to drink?

Identify water and minerals, then play the microbial-warning change and pause on the gray crossed symbols. Keep the same progress when switching to chemical contamination: purple diamonds remain. Green outlines mean the heated disinfectant residual is not modeled.

## Clarity is not purity or a test result

Water may contain dissolved minerals such as calcium and magnesium that are invisible to the eye. Some supply systems maintain regulated disinfectant residuals to reduce microbial risk in distribution. Treatment varies locally. Color, smell and clarity do not replace testing, and chlorine odor alone cannot establish safety.

## Boiling addresses microbial hazards

Boiling according to applicable instructions can inactivate disease-causing microorganisms; it does not physically filter them out. Water must also be protected from recontamination while cooling and stored in clean, covered containers. Local instructions can differ, and the on-screen button is not an actual treatment procedure. The continuous transition links the before and after concepts while keeping the same symbols. Intermediate frames have no assigned temperature, duration or surviving fraction, so progress cannot establish real water safety.

## Chemical contamination needs another response

Boiling does not reduce lead and cannot make water contaminated with fuels, toxic chemicals or radioactive materials safe. It does not remove every dissolved mineral either. Follow local instructions about safe alternative supplies and contaminant-specific treatment; a general purification claim does not prove removal of a particular chemical.

## A common misconception

Boiling does not produce pure water and is not a universal remedy for chemical contamination.

## Model limits

Particles show categories and treatment differences, not counts or test results. Progress and fading are not a boiling timer, temperature or microbial survival curve; heated disinfectant residuals are not modeled. Compare only on screen; adults should follow local water advice.

### What is in clear water?

Look at this clear glass. Most of it is water, but small amounts of minerals may be dissolved in it, rather like salt that disappears from view after dissolving. We use different symbols to show these categories. They are not really this large, and our eyes cannot directly see them this way.

*Scene cue: Select water and then minerals, highlighting their symbols.*

### Check local advice

Tap-water advice differs from place to place, so first let an adult check the local supply information. If a notice warns about microbes, adults may need to boil water as directed. Compare the pictures on screen. What changes in the pathogen symbols after the modeled treatment?

*Scene cue: Choose the microbial warning, play from 0 to about 60%, then pause with the same water and mineral symbols.*

### Inactivated, not removed

The gray symbols gain a line across them. This means the modeled pathogens have lost their ability to cause infection, not that a tiny net removed them. Mineral symbols remain. Pause, then try the purple chemical-contamination scenario. Those purple symbols stay after boiling too.

*Scene cue: Advance to 100%, switch to chemical contamination without resetting, then scrub back to confirm persistent purple symbols.*

### Boiling has limits

Boiling helps with some problems but not all. It cannot remove lead from water, for example. If a notice warns of such contamination, an adult should obtain the safe water source it recommends. Clear-looking water is not proof of safety. We are only observing on screen; real boiling and pouring hot water are jobs for adults.

*Scene cue: Hold the chemical-warning result and emphasize that the screen is not a boiling timer or safety certificate.*
