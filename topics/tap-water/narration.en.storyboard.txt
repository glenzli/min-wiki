01 · What is in clear water?
[Select water and then minerals, highlighting their symbols.]

Look at this clear glass. Most of it is water, but small amounts of minerals may be dissolved in it, rather like salt that disappears from view after dissolving. We use different symbols to show these categories. They are not really this large, and our eyes cannot directly see them this way.

---

02 · Check local advice
[Choose the microbial warning, play from 0 to about 60%, then pause with the same water and mineral symbols.]

Tap-water advice differs from place to place, so first let an adult check the local supply information. If a notice warns about microbes, adults may need to boil water as directed. Compare the pictures on screen. What changes in the pathogen symbols after the modeled treatment?

---

03 · Inactivated, not removed
[Advance to 100%, switch to chemical contamination without resetting, then scrub back to confirm persistent purple symbols.]

The gray symbols gain a line across them. This means the modeled pathogens have lost their ability to cause infection, not that a tiny net removed them. Mineral symbols remain. Pause, then try the purple chemical-contamination scenario. Those purple symbols stay after boiling too.

---

04 · Boiling has limits
[Hold the chemical-warning result and emphasize that the screen is not a boiling timer or safety certificate.]

Boiling helps with some problems but not all. It cannot remove lead from water, for example. If a notice warns of such contamination, an adult should obtain the safe water source it recommends. Clear-looking water is not proof of safety. We are only observing on screen; real boiling and pouring hot water are jobs for adults.
