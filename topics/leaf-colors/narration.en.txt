Remember this branch. A small leaf unfolds from its bud. Its edge expands while its stalk stays at the same place. We are following one leaf, not turning four unrelated pictures. When it has expanded, move closer to discover its work.

The leaf is connected to the whole plant. Water enters the roots and travels through xylem to the veins. Some evaporates inside the leaf and diffuses through stomata. A filled marker becoming hollow indicates a change of phase, not drops spraying from a pore. In light, mesophyll cells also use light energy to make sugars. Living cells respire during both day and night.

Move towards senescence. The tree recovers some nutrients, green chlorophyll decreases and existing yellow becomes easier to see. Some leaves also make anthocyanins that accumulate in vacuoles and give red colours. These are different routes; not every leaf goes from yellow to red. Enter the same patch and locate its pigments.

The connection between stalk and branch weakens and the leaf detaches. Its scar remains, and the same leaf is still visible. It no longer receives that root-supplied water flow. Real litter later dries, fragments and is used by decomposers; this model stops at detachment. Next year's leaf is new, not this old leaf turning back into a bud.
