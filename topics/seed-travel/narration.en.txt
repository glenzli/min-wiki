A plant stays rooted, yet its offspring can travel. Seeds may ride the wind or animals. A dandelion’s white parachute carries a tiny fruit containing a seed. It has no flapping wings or little motor. What do you think happens when there is no wind?

Launch it and watch it descend without wind. Pause, then try a breeze. The parachute slows its fall, giving moving air more time to carry it sideways. Stronger wind changes the route, but real air also swirls and rises, so one setting cannot tell us an exact landing place.

Meet burdock next. Instead of a white parachute, it has little hooks that may catch on passing fur. As the animal moves in our picture, the attached structure moves too. It may later fall off elsewhere. Riding fur and riding wind are two different ways to travel. Watch three connected events: contact with fur, a ride, and release of the same burr. It is never replaced with another burr, and landing does not instantly produce a seedling.

Reaching a new place is only part of the story. A seed needs suitable germination conditions, and the seedling needs a place where it can survive. Landing on stone can differ from landing in suitable soil. If a burr catches your clothes, inspect its hooks with a grown-up and ask whose ride it used.
