Without wind, can a dandelion parachute fly sideways by itself?

Compare no wind, light wind and stronger wind: the same dandelion fruit settles while horizontal transport changes. Choose burdock and pause at contact with fur, attached travel and release. Scrub to any moment.

## The travelling unit is not always a bare seed

A dandelion disperses a small fruit containing a seed, with a stalk and pappus. Burdock’s hooked structures attach to fur or clothing and transport enclosed fruits. Everyday language calls them seeds, but the seed, surrounding fruit and dispersal structures have different anatomical identities.

## A pappus slows descent while wind transports it

Gravity pulls the fruit downward. Air interaction with the porous pappus slows settling, allowing wind more transport time. Experiments reveal a separated vortex ring above the pappus. Release height, turbulence, moisture and terrain also matter, so the wind settings cannot predict a fixed distance or landing point.

## Transport is only one step towards establishment

Hooked structures must contact and attach to a suitable surface, travel and eventually detach. Arrival does not ensure germination. Dispersal changes exposure to resources, competitors and enemies, but farther is not always better. Transport, germination and survival to adulthood are separate outcomes.

## A common misconception

The parachute is not an engine: it slows descent, while moving air provides transport. Travelling far does not guarantee a new plant.

## Model limits

The 18-second illustrative journey predicts neither wind fields, attachment strength nor landing position. Dandelions share a qualitative settling curve; burr release time is chosen for explanation. Landing does not automatically trigger germination.

### Plants that cannot walk

A plant stays rooted, yet its offspring can travel. Seeds may ride the wind or animals. A dandelion’s white parachute carries a tiny fruit containing a seed. It has no flapping wings or little motor. What do you think happens when there is no wind?

*Scene cue: Choose the dandelion with no wind.*

### Try a breeze

Launch it and watch it descend without wind. Pause, then try a breeze. The parachute slows its fall, giving moving air more time to carry it sideways. Stronger wind changes the route, but real air also swirls and rises, so one setting cannot tell us an exact landing place.

*Scene cue: Compare still-air and light-wind paths with a pause.*

### A ride using hooks

Meet burdock next. Instead of a white parachute, it has little hooks that may catch on passing fur. As the animal moves in our picture, the attached structure moves too. It may later fall off elsewhere. Riding fur and riding wind are two different ways to travel. Watch three connected events: contact with fur, a ride, and release of the same burr. It is never replaced with another burr, and landing does not instantly produce a seedling.

*Scene cue: Pause near one-fifth progress for contact, midway for transport with fur, and at the end for the same dispersal unit reaching the ground.*

### Arrival is not the end

Reaching a new place is only part of the story. A seed needs suitable germination conditions, and the seedling needs a place where it can survive. Landing on stone can differ from landing in suitable soil. If a burr catches your clothes, inspect its hooks with a grown-up and ask whose ride it used.

*Scene cue: End after transport without showing guaranteed germination.*
