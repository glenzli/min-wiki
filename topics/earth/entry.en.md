A rock, a breath of air and a drop of seawater can each open a way into Earth. This digital museum investigates the same planet throughout: formed about 4.54 billion years ago, it continues to change through internal heat, sunlight, gravity and life. [NIST: Earth's age and natural clocks](https://www.nist.gov/how-do-you-measure-it/how-do-you-know-age-fossils-and-other-old-things)

Begin with a question, then follow the evidence deeper. Each of the eight halls has its own substantial exploration and bilingual reading. This guide, the hall explanations, limits and narration remain readable without running the interactions.

## How do the halls form a connected journey?

**Begin inside to understand why the surface moves.** Crust, mantle and core mainly distinguish composition. Lithosphere includes the crust and relatively rigid uppermost mantle. Most mantle material is solid yet can slowly deform over long periods. Interior brings scale and seismic evidence together to investigate this hidden structure. [USGS: what lies below plates?](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-tectonic-plates-floating-magma)

**Follow time to see environments and life change together.** Oxygen-producing microbes long preceded land forests. Respiration, decomposition and chemical reactions consumed their oxygen; production did not immediately mean atmospheric accumulation. History places this budget on a long time scale and connects it with animals and environments. [Research on the Great Oxidation](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1608824114)

**Pick up a rock, then enter its minerals.** Rock groups describe formation processes; minerals have characteristic composition and structure. Ore concerns extracting useful constituents under particular conditions. There is no required rock-cycle circuit, and color cannot certify specimen identity. The two halls progress from visible grains to formation conditions and microscopic explanations. [USGS: rocks and minerals](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-a-rock-and-a-mineral)

**Let water, ice and air reconnect the whole planet.** Glacier change compares accumulation with losses; seawater density requires temperature and salinity together. Climate investigates past evidence and feedbacks. Systems distinguishes causes: night follows rotation out of sunlight, whereas auroras emit light through upper-atmosphere particle interactions. Connected phenomena need separate mechanisms. [NOAA thermohaline circulation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_currents/05conveyor1.html), [NASA auroras](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/aurora/en/)

**Life branches, and ores have different origins.** Ancient Life places microbes, seafloor communities and terrestrial forests in their environments while preserving uncertainty and fossil-record bias. Ores follows retained material through fractures, weathering and sorting. Direct structural evidence, comparative inference and artistic completion are distinguished in reconstructions.

Earthquakes do not shake the mantle into melt, and a rift need not erupt or become an ocean. Geological progress, the fault event and the camera are separate.

Enter the [global ice-edge, region and sample route](../earth-climate/entry.en.md?earth.climate.view=journey) and retain site A as the ice edge passes it. Compare the coast and reflection while the local accumulation/ablation budget has its own replay. Seawater freezing and snow-derived land ice have separate sources. Both routes support explicit play, pause, scrubbing and reset. Display seconds compress the explanation; shared position and stage do not merge million-year changes with a seconds-scale event into one physical clock.

## From one planet into eight connected halls

Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago. Interior connects compositional and mechanical layers with seismic evidence. History follows coevolution on a linear time scale. Rocks and Minerals distinguish formation processes, crystal structure, specimen properties and ore uses. Climate and Systems compare ice, water, air, sunlight and internal activity. These entrances investigate the same planet without sharing one process clock. Ancient Life separates body fossils, traces, chemical evidence and reconstruction; Ores connects formation, occurrence, structure and uses. Galleries select evidence-backed examples, not every species or deposit.

## Material moves; budgets determine what remains

Rock material can stay or undergo weathering, transport, metamorphism and melting followed by crystallization; no complete circuit is required. Oxygen-producing life likewise does not immediately create atmospheric accumulation. Respiration, decomposition and chemical reactions consume oxygen, and a production surplus can remain. Glacier change also compares accumulation with losses. Such budgets explain mechanisms without completely reconstructing Earth's history.

## Follow observations into evidence while keeping model limits

Specimens, seismic records, isotopes and ice cores provide different evidence. Deep structure and past environments require several lines of inference. Cutaways, lattices, time windows and circulation arrows are representations with stated scales and conditions. Seawater density depends on temperature and salinity together; daylight and auroras have different causes. Inverse-square distance calculates relative sunlight alone, rather than temperature or a habitability score.

## A common misconception

Earth is not a pot of magma carrying a thin crust, and continents are not solid blocks of granite. Rocks have no compulsory three-stage circuit. The Great Oxidation was not the instant arrival of plants or today's roughly 21% atmospheric oxygen, and an iron-rich band alone is not unique proof of it. Cold water is not denser than every possible warm sample: compare salinity too. Auroras are not sunlight illuminating the night sky. A magnetic field, a moon, mass or orbital distance is not a single habitability switch.

## Model limits

Observations, inferences supported by several kinds of evidence and teaching illustrations are distinguished. Interior circles share a radius scale; the thin-layer enlargement has a separate depth scale. Seismic paths explain basic principal waves and shadows, without solving real velocities, travel times or the full set of phases. Plate and rock progress does not represent actual years. Timeline markers give approximate ages of selected evidence, not precise birthdays of life or processes. Oxygen budgets, material markers, seawater layering, circulation arrows, field lines and auroras explain mechanisms, rather than predicting ancient climate, global currents or space weather. The only quantitative habitability calculation is inverse-square sunlight at fixed solar luminosity; other comparisons describe evidence and unknowns. Chapters do not share one physical clock. Mineral structure and property models do not certify real specimens. Glacier and climate comparisons explain budgets and feedbacks without forecasting ice ages or future temperatures. Ancient communities and plants are reconstructions with stated evidence levels, not ecological snapshots. Preservation, sampling and ore-sorting use teaching rules, not calibrated probabilities, processing or exploration forecasts. Global breakup and ice edges are idealized mechanism illustrations, not paleogeographic reconstructions. Fault slip and magma form separate observation branches; sea ice and snow-derived land ice have different sources. Retaining identity across scales does not give them one physical clock.

## Narration {#narration}

[Download spoken text](narration.en.txt?download) · [Download storyboard](narration.en.storyboard.txt?download)

### A thin surface above a deep interior

The rock beneath our feet feels hard, yet Earth keeps changing. Its outer crust is thin. Beneath it lie a thick rocky mantle and a metallic core. Most of the mantle is solid, but over a very long time it can slowly deform. We have never drilled to the center. Seismic waves and many other clues help us investigate those hidden depths.

*Scene cue: Show the common-radius cutaway, then the independently scaled thin-layer enlargement. Compare P and S waves, and distinguish samples from inferences.*

### The air has a history too

Long ago, Earth's air contained far less oxygen than it does today. Some microbes used sunlight to make food and release oxygen. Other living processes and chemical reactions consumed it. More oxygen could remain only when production exceeded consumption. This change took a long time and altered the environments in which life could live.

*Scene cue: Read the linear 4.54-billion-year overview before opening the Great Oxidation window. Compare oxygen production without a retained surplus against lower consumption that allows oxygen to remain.*

### Travel with the material

A rock can stay at the surface, or wind and water can break it into particles and move them elsewhere. Those particles may slowly become a new rock. Buried material can change while still solid. If it melts and later cools, it takes another route. There is no required lap around a rock cycle. Follow the material and ask how changing conditions change its form. Looking closer at minerals raises more questions: how is a crystal arranged inside, and what evidence identifies a specimen?

*Scene cue: Keep the same material markers. Try staying, then weathering, transport and cementation. Step back and compare solid-state metamorphism with melting followed by two cooling routes. Connect to structure and property evidence in Minerals without identifying an unknown object from a photograph alone.*

### Earth is still active today

Temperature and salt together affect seawater density. Glacier change also depends on a budget of accumulation and loss. Earth's rotation turns the same surface site toward and away from the Sun; auroras have another cause, involving particles interacting high in the air. Many conditions shape our home together. When one changes, look for evidence and ask what remains unknown.

*Scene cue: Compare equal-volume seawater samples A and B. Follow daylight at the same surface station; treat magnetic-field and particle layers as a separate causal explanation. End by comparing evidence, inferences and unknowns in habitability cases. Compare glacier budgets and evidence in Climate, distinguishing past climate changes from current rapid warming.*
