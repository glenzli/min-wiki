## Diagram and material provenance

- `scene.ts`: original Canvas cross-section, continuously transformed grain polygons, fixed origin markers, mineral illustrations and cooling comparison. They are teaching geometry, not images of geological samples or measured atomic structures.
- `specimenGeometry.ts`: original fractured hand-specimen geometry, obtained by intersecting radial directions with irregular planes, adding bounded relief, then merging/smoothing the surface. Slate uses its own thin-slab morphology. Other specimens vary fracture boundaries deterministically.
- `specimen3d.ts`: original seamless spatial noise/cellular materials for interlocking granitic crystals, fine basaltic groundmass and shallow pores, granular sandstone/bedding, carbonate inclusions, slate cleavage, marble grains/veins and gneissic bands. These are representative illustrations, not scans, geological reconstructions, mineral maps, or real physical grain-size measurements. Carbonate inclusions are stylized, not identifiable fossil species.
- Three.js and the repository's shared `threeStage.ts` provide rendering and controls; there are no downloaded textures, exhibition assets or external 3D models. No third-party specimen image license is required by these original assets. Three.js retains its dependency license.

## Model boundaries

The 24 markers retain a chosen material origin, not immutable mineral crystals or individual atoms. Chemical exchange, weathering loss, sorting, partial melt partitioning, and added cement are not solved. Progress interpolates a selected illustration and never measures elapsed time. Process buttons describe necessary conditions but do not compute temperature, pressure, reaction kinetics, stress or plate velocities. The same silicic composition is held for the qualitative granite/rhyolite comparison; the carbonate and basalt specimens are separate examples. Not all continents are granite. No displayed specimen is described as collected from Earth's core. Schist is not tuff.
