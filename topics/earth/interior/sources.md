| [USGS Volcano Hazards Program — Components of Igneous Rocks](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/components-igneous-rocks) | Accessible explanation of cooling, grain growth, fine-grained/groundmass versus coarse-grained textures and basaltic plutonic equivalents. Only original prose interpretation is used, without the published image. The P1 display is generic basaltic granular material, not a mineral identification, quartz cluster or atomic lattice. |

All twenty P1 identities, the four melt-capable choices, stage thresholds, F1 displacement field and 3/18-second playback durations are **authored teaching choices**. The only balance claimed is the explicitly defined twenty arbitrary material portions: residue + melt + crystallized portions. These are not calibrated mass, real mineral proportions, a 20% melt estimate, a pressure/temperature solution or an earthquake forecast. The local F1 event never drives P1 melting. Global and regional late water are the same specified sea-connection boundary condition; sea level and water entry are not solved. All rift meshes, Voronoi domains and textures are original code-generated assets and add no externally licensed imagery.

- Whole-Earth circles use one common linear radius mapping. A 30 km crust is less than one pixel thick at a 171 px Earth radius; it is not given a minimum visible physical thickness. The depth locator is a nonmetric marker.
- A separate linear **0–300 km** inset enlarges vertical thickness. Widths are illustrative. The inset marks the very same surface-to-depth interval, not a second independent planet.
- Lithosphere and composition are overlapping classifications. The 100 km example crosses the crust–mantle boundary; it is not a fifth material shell between crust and mantle.
- Plates A/B retain identity and distinct crust / lithospheric-mantle bands in cross-sections. The transform view switches explicitly to a plan view, so screen-vertical offset means horizontal sliding on Earth.
- Each boundary has different specified prerequisites. Progress is a deterministic teaching coordinate, **not elapsed geological time**, a stress solver, a mass-balance solver, or an earthquake/eruption forecast.
- “No relative motion” turns off only the two illustrated plates' relative motion and retains the user's selected progress; it does not claim a real tectonically quiet location has no other processes.
- Inner-core solidity is not inferred from outer-core S-wave absence alone. The explanation names additional inner-core-sensitive waves, whole-Earth vibrations and high-pressure material constraints; it does not display invented measured records.
