# Earth-history sources and boundaries

Checked on 2026-10-25 through actual web search/open/find calls. Explanations are original paraphrases. No exhibition photograph, museum illustration or long source passage is reproduced. The authored SVG/HTML diagrams require no external image licence.

## Clocks, early Earth and life

| Specific source | Claims used / qualifications |
| --- | --- |
| [NASA — Facts about Earth](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/) | Formation by accretion about 4.5 Ga and differentiated present structure. Not used alone to assign the more precise 4.54 Ga age. |
| [NIST — How Do You Know the Age of Fossils and Other Old Things?](https://www.nist.gov/how-do-you-measure-it/how-do-you-know-age-fossils-and-other-old-things) | U isotope clocks, terrestrial/lunar/meteoritic minerals, Earth 4.54 Ga ± about 50 Ma, C-14 for much more recent remains. No carbon dating of Earth's age. |
| [AMNH — Formation of Our Solar System](https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/the-universe/planets/formation-of-our-solar-system) | Accretion, melting and material separation into metal-rich cores and rocky outer regions. The 4500 Ma signpost is illustrative, not a dated completion of differentiation or the modern inner core. |
| [NASA — Moon Formation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/formation/) | Apollo sample evidence, high-temperature history, Earth/Moon compositional comparisons and the leading giant-impact hypothesis. Exact impact timing/conditions are not fixed in the study. |
| [Wilde et al. (2001), Nature — Evidence from detrital zircons…](https://www.nature.com/articles/35051550) | Jack Hills zircon 4404 ± 8 Ma; chemistry and oxygen isotopes interpreted as involving surface material altered by a liquid hydrosphere. An indirect water-interaction inference, not a 4.4 Ga life detection or ocean map. Abstract and figures' descriptions were accessible; no subscription-only full-text claims are used. |
| [Djokic et al. (2017), Nature Communications — Earliest signs of life on land…](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15263) / [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5436104/) | Dresser Formation microbial/thermal-spring records around 3.48 Ga, rounded to an evidence-by-3500-Ma signpost. Search record was accessible; direct publisher/PMC fetches intermittently returned auth/CAPTCHA. Cross-checked the broad 3.5 Ga stromatolite claim against the Smithsonian source below. No claim that this is the exact origin of life. |
| [Smithsonian — Early Life on Earth: Animal Origins](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/life-science/early-life-earth-animal-origins) | Microbial records, stromatolites, cellular cooperation and Ediacaran communities by about 580 Ma. Some language and dates on this general-audience page are stronger/older than used here: the study does not repeat “first photosynthesizers”, instantaneous oxygenation, or its old 541 Ma Cambrian boundary. |
| [Gibson et al. (2018), Geology — Precise age of Bangiomorpha pubescens…](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/7brz9-8mw86) | Original abstract: eukaryotic record by 1.9–1.4 Ga; Bangiomorpha bracketed age 1.047 +0.013/−0.017 Ga. The study uses 1800 Ma as a broad eukaryotic signpost and 1047 Ma for this specific multicellular red alga. Neither is a universal first-life/first-animal birthday. |
| [ICS — International Chronostratigraphic Chart, 2026/06 PDF](https://stratigraphy.org/ICSchart/ChronostratChart2026-06.pdf) | Selected version fixed for traceability; actual one-page chart opened and parsed. Period bases shown in the deeper table: Cambrian 538.8 ± 0.6, Ordovician 486.85 ± 1.5, Silurian 443.1 ± 0.9, Devonian 419.62 ± 1.36, Carboniferous 358.86 ± 0.19, Triassic 251.902 ± 0.024, Jurassic 201.4 ± 0.2 Ma; Paleogene 66 Ma has no listed ±. Chart Hadean base 4567 Ma is not Earth's exact birthday. Archean/Eoarchean 4031 ± 3 Ma, Proterozoic 2500 Ma and Quaternary 2.58 Ma remain distinct from the 4540 Ma whole-Earth age estimate. Family event ages are rounded, not substituted for dated fossil assemblages or event durations. |

## Oxygen and rock records

| Specific source | Claims used / qualifications |
| --- | --- |
| [Gumsley et al. (2017), PNAS — Timing and tempo of the Great Oxidation Event](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1608824114) | U–Pb chronology, onset and repeated oxygenation changes, glaciations and a broad transition continuing toward about 2060 Ma. Teaching context rounded to 2400–2100 Ma. Start/end and stratigraphic correlations remain debated; no instantaneous jump to modern oxygen. |
| [Ozaki et al. (2019), Nature Communications — Anoxygenic photosynthesis and delayed oxygenation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10872-z) | Original experiments/genomics/model work: early oxygenic producers need not imply a persistently oxygenated atmosphere; iron, nutrient competition and linked cycles complicate the history. The simple ledger is not a reproduction or numerical calibration of this paper's Earth-system model. |
| [AMNH — Banded Iron Formation](https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/planet-earth/how-has-the-earth-evolved/banded-iron-formation) | Museum specimen explanation of produced oxygen reacting with dissolved iron and depositing iron minerals. Used as one pathway, not a universal origin or direct global air-oxygen gauge. |
| [NASA Astrobiology — The Biology Behind Banded Iron Formations](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/the-biology-behind-banded-iron-formations/) | Research-linked account of Fe redox records, reduced-species consumption and evidence for oxygen-independent photoferrotrophic iron oxidation in ancient Isua deposits. This qualification is preserved in both languages. |

The ledger's `8`, `50%`, `6`, fixed comparison demand `2`, 0–24 turns and all unit bounds are **authored teaching choices**, not inferred ancient atmospheric fluxes, percentages of present atmospheric oxygen, or geological years. Oxygen is conserved only inside this explicit budget: cumulative release = aerobic recycling + actual geological consumption + retained free oxygen. Geological demand is renewed, not depleted to force an eventual outcome.

## Animals, forests, land and crises

| Specific source | Claims used / qualifications |
| --- | --- |
| [Smithsonian — Cambrian Explosion with Karma Nanglu](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/paleontology/cambrian-explosion-life-paleontologist-karma-nanglu) | Changing animal fossil record and how researchers study it. The study explains extended diversification and preservation bias, rather than a one-day invention of all animals. Boundary age comes from ICS. |
| [Wellman, Osterloff & Mohiuddin (2003), Nature — Fragments of the earliest land plants](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/106/1/wellmanch1.pdf) | Original deposited paper: Ordovician spore-bearing fragments; dispersed spores earlier than large plant fossils by roughly 50 Ma. The near-470-Ma signpost is evidence of early land plants, not the first terrestrial life. |
| [National Museums Scotland — The mysterious 15-million-year gap in our evolution](https://www.nms.ac.uk/discover-catalogue/the-mysterious-15-million-year-gap-in-our-evolution) | Museum fossil evidence for early plant spores/arthropods and the water–land transition of tetrapods. Uses approximately 425 Ma arthropod and 365 Ma transition signposts, without guaranteeing a precisely dated first land animal. Digits alone do not establish terrestrial walking. |
| [Natural History Museum — How trees changed the world](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/how-trees-changed-the-world.html) | Plant carbon storage, forest environments, peat/burial and coal; explicitly qualified so forests do not inevitably become coal. |
| [Natural History Museum — The fossil tree](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/the-fossil-tree.html) | Additional collection context for Devonian trees; not the current timeline's Cairo evidence. The newer [Somerset forest account](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2024/march/earliest-fossilised-forest-discovered-in-somerset.html) was also opened to avoid an outdated “oldest” claim. |
| [Cardiff research team — Cairo forest discovery](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/news/view/1739669-scientists-uncover-worlds-oldest-forest) / [Stein et al. (2020), Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(19)31569-6) | The team describes approximately 386 Ma fossil soils and mapped, in-place root networks. These are distinguished from canopy reconstruction. The 2019 release's “oldest” headline is not adopted. Publisher fetch returned 403; claims use the accessible institutional team's account, cross-checked against [Binghamton's account](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/2210/modern-trees-emerged-earlier-than-previously-believed-new-research-reveals), which rounds the site to 385 Ma. This is not a later Carboniferous coal-swamp reconstruction. |
| [Nelsen et al. (2016), PNAS — Delayed fungal evolution did not cause the Paleozoic peak in coal production](https://repository.si.edu/items/52e46b6c-12ec-44d5-8ce6-160781d13956) | Original paper's institutional abstract rejects the simple lignin/fungal-evolution-lag explanation. Depositional and climatic conditions are part of the explanation; forest presence does not guarantee coal. |
| [Royal Ontario Museum — Waptia evidence](https://www.rom.on.ca/news-releases/burgess-shale-fossil-site-reveals-oldest-evidence-brood-care) | Research team's approximately 508 Ma Burgess Shale specimen with preserved eggs/embryos supplies a concrete example of a fossil-community age much younger than the Cambrian base. No museum image or “oldest care” superlative is reused. |
| [Smithsonian — Extinction Over Time](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/paleontology/extinction-over-time) | Different extinction histories and broad volcanic/impact/glaciation mechanisms. Its older numerical labels 199/447/378 Ma are not copied: selected ICS estimates distinguish boundaries from event intervals. End-Ordovician context spans about 445–443 Ma; the Late Devonian has pulses around 372 and 359 Ma, not one uniform 13-Ma extinction. No fixed kill percentages or universal single-day disasters are asserted. |
| [Da Silva et al. (2020), Scientific Reports — Timing and phased ocean anoxia in the Kellwasser events](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7395115/) | Geochronological and geochemical evidence for phased Late Devonian marine oxygen loss. Used for the Kellwasser example, not as a single causal model for all Kellwasser and Hangenberg crises or proof that forests alone caused extinction. |
| [Nicholls et al. (2026), npj Biodiversity — Timing and nature of marine ecosystem recovery following the Permian–Triassic mass extinction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44185-025-00117-2) | A scholarly Perspective, explicitly not new primary field data: Siberian Traps environmental consequences include warming, anoxia and acidification; ecological recovery timing and function vary. Its cited early complex ecosystems do not establish global complete recovery. No percentage denominator or fixed recovery clock is borrowed. Publication date is 2026 despite the DOI URL's 2025 segment. |
| [Capriolo et al. (2020), Nature Communications — Deep CO₂ in end-Triassic CAMP](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-15325-6) | Original melt-inclusion evidence of abundant CO₂ in CAMP basalt, supporting substantial carbon release and consequent warming/acidification. Does not by itself settle every causal step or make all large igneous provinces identical. |
| [Hull et al. (2020), Science — On impact and volcanism across the K–Pg boundary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31949074/) / [NASA-supported research account](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/impacts-and-volcanism-at-the-end-cretaceous/) | Timing, carbon-cycle modelling and temperature evidence distinguish impact from volcanic environmental background. The impact has strong converging extinction evidence; contemporaneous volcanism is not automatically given equal causal weight. The accessible NASA research-program account was opened; PubMed retrieval was intermittent. |
| [Smithsonian — Mass Extinction: Solving the Dinosaur Mystery](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/paleontology/mass-extinction-solving-dinosaur-mystery) | Evidence-based reconstruction of the 66 Ma crisis. The study specifies non-avian dinosaurs and surviving bird lineages. |
| [Smithsonian Human Origins — Homo sapiens](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-sapiens) | Approximately 300 ka history for our species, African origins, not all hominins or all of genus Homo. |

## Ice-age contexts and recent history

| Specific source | Claims used / qualifications |
| --- | --- |
| [Subcommission on Cryogenian Stratigraphy](https://cryogenian.stratigraphy.org/) | The official commission specifies a broad Cryogenian framework c.720–c.635 Ma. This is a period context, not continuous global ice. |
| [NASA GISS — Snowball Earth Might Have Been Slushy](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/research/features/201508_slushball/) | Research-group account of low-latitude glacial evidence, ice-reflectivity feedback and hard-snowball/slushball tests. Used for evidence and open questions; its approximate old durations do not date the entire Cryogenian band. |
| [Montañez & Poulsen (2013), Annual Review — The Late Paleozoic Ice Age](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/10.1146/annurev.earth.031208.100118) | Specialist synthesis, explicitly a review: dynamic regional ice centres, contractions/warm intervals, CO₂ and multiple feedbacks. Broad context 360–260 Ma, not unbroken global glaciation. |
| [Barr et al. (2022), Nature Communications — 60 million years of glaciation in the Transantarctic Mountains](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-33310-z) | Original reconstruction distinguishes older mountain glaciers from major ice-sheet growth around 34 Ma and subsequent changing ice conditions. |
| [NPS — Quaternary Period, 2.58 Ma to today](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/quaternary-period.htm) | Repeated glacial cycles, sea-level/land connections and regionally varying large-animal losses with several possible contributors. The outdated ~195 ka Homo sapiens text on this page is not used; the Smithsonian human source governs that date. |
| [USGS — Glacier extent and sea level at the LGM](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-does-present-glacier-extent-and-sea-level-compare-extent-glaciers-and-global-sea-level) | Last Glacial Maximum summarized as about 20 ka, expanded land ice and much lower sea level. The cursor signpost uses 21 ka within the approximate peak interval, not a globally simultaneous local temperature minimum. |

Huronian context uses the same broadly 2400–2100 Ma temporal neighbourhood as the oxygen/glaciation paper, with a 2300 Ma route signpost. Climate and oxygenation links are hypotheses/mechanisms constrained by records, not asserted as one settled single cause. The 12 ka megafauna signpost is an entry into regionally asynchronous late-glacial/recent history, not an extinction date for every mammoth population.
