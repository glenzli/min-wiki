Hydrangeas can look like soft clouds of blue, pink or white. You may hear that changing the soil changes their colour. That can happen, but several conditions matter. Today we will grow a new bloom in our picture and compare what happens under different settings.

Keep the cultivar the same, adjust the soil pH and grow a bloom. Pause and remember its colour. Next change only the aluminium supply and grow another. Aluminium here is something the plant can obtain from soil, not blue paint. Soil conditions influence how available it is.

First follow the roots and transport. Gold dots represent available aluminium, traveling through roots and stem toward a sepal. Inside a cell, pink ovals represent anthocyanin and green squares are helper pigments. Together with aluminium they can form a blue complex. These little shapes are identification symbols. Uptake, transport and new growth take time; garden flowers do not instantly change their clothes.

Try the white cultivar. It does not follow the same route to blue because the needed coloured pigment is missing. Colour depends on the plant as well as its surroundings. The useful habit in our experiment is changing one thing, waiting and comparing carefully before deciding what the result means.
