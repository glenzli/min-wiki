01 · Meet the travelers
[Show the distant pass and wait for approach.]

Let’s travel with a star. A star is a huge ball of hot gas held together by its own gravity. The black hole nearby has a special boundary: once something crosses it inward, even light cannot escape. But passing nearby does not always mean falling in. First watch the farther route and see whether the star continues on its way.

---

02 · Different pulls
[Pause for about four seconds when outer layers first detach.]

Now bring the route closer. The side facing the black hole feels a different pull from the far side. A large enough difference can pull the star apart. Pause for a moment. Does the whole star vanish together, or do some outer layers leave first? Take your time and follow the flowing material.

---

03 · Returning is another part of the journey
[Use the top view; follow the tail, returning streams and selected recent paths.]

Keep watching as the material becomes a long, curved stream. Some travels away. Some takes a long route and comes back. Returning does not mean falling straight in or already becoming a disk. Follow the streams from above: which part is moving away, and which is returning? Faint lines mark the recent paths of a few gas parcels. They are not real rings growing around the black hole.

---

04 · Why the bright patches change
[Inspect local patches and fading in the close-up, then use the top view to locate the remaining gas.]

Use the black hole close-up and find the orange patches. Real streams may collide and heat up when they meet. The changing glow helps us imagine this process, but its colors cannot tell us an exact temperature. A fading patch does not always mean the gas has fallen in. Check whether the gas is still there, then return to the overview: the long tail and curved streams are continuing their journey outside.
