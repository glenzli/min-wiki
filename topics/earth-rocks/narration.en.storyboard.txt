01 · What else is inside a rock?
[Compare granite with enlarged quartz, feldspar and mica illustrations; explain that color alone cannot determine identity.]

Granite can contain several different minerals. Quartz, feldspar and mica are not named differently just because their pieces differ in size. Mineral structure and composition connect with another question: how did the whole rock form?

---

02 · Water and wind move particles
[Stop at loose hillslope particles before showing transport to a basin and lithification. Distinguish weathering, transport and deposition.]

Rock can change slowly in place, or break into particles carried away by water, wind or gravity. After settling elsewhere, burial, compaction and cementation may turn the particles into new sedimentary rock.

---

03 · Change while solid, or melt first
[Branch from the same buried state into metamorphism or melting, then compare cooling routes for one melt composition.]

Buried rock sometimes remains solid while its minerals and structure change. Other conditions can cause melting. When that melt cools, it crystallizes into rock again. These routes involve different processes.

---

04 · There is no required destination
[Retain the same material markers, compare staying and branching, and connect to structure, properties and evidence in the Minerals hall.]

Material with the same ancestry can stay for a long time or be uplifted, weathered and buried again. We trace its provenance without promising that every original crystal survives. Before recording the next step, ask whether its conditions really changed.
