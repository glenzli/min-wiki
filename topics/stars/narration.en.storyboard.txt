01 · One cloud, different futures
[Choose each mass path and scrub through the main sequence, expanded envelope and remnant; compare the former-envelope outline, right-hand size change and endpoint readouts.]

A cold cloud is mostly gas with a little dust. A dense part contracts; gas falls through a disk toward its young center and some flows out at the poles. Contraction and accretion make the center glow first. Once its core sustains hydrogen fusion, it joins the main sequence. Different birth masses can lead to different lifetimes and endings.

02 · One star grows into a giant
[Scrub back and forward across the Sun-like red-giant stage; watch the radius and Mercury/Venus states.]

Late in a Sun-like star's life, changes in the core expand its envelope. Keep Mercury’s and Venus’s orbits on screen and watch when they enter that envelope. Earth's outcome also depends on mass loss, orbital changes and tides, so one circle diagram is not enough.

03 · Why it shines and sometimes erupts
[Select core, transport, photosphere, spots, flare and wind in order; locate each in the scene.]

Long-lasting solar light and heat come mainly from core fusion. Energy crosses radiative and convective regions to the photosphere. Surface magnetic fields make darker sunspots and briefly release stored energy as flares; the corona also sends out a continuing solar wind. These processes have different causes and timescales.

04 · Orbiting together
[Switch among all three complete systems and name each planet's host; then compare the figure eight, triangle and wandering case with the perturbation overlay.]

A planet need not orbit a lone Sun. Kepler-47's three planets circle a stellar pair; another planet can circle just one star in a binary; KOI-5's planet circles A in a triple. Then compare three fixed equal-mass mathematical starts: a figure eight, rotating triangle and wandering path. Gravity is integrated step by step; none reproduces those observed systems, and the wandering is not driven by random forces. Crossing trails do not establish a collision.
