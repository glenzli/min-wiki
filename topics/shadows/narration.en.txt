When the lamp shines, a darker patch appears beside the puppet. It is not a piece of black cloth dropped by the puppet. The puppet blocks some light from this lamp, so part of the ground receives less illumination. Find the lamp, puppet and shadow and notice their positions.

Keep the lamp from moving left or right and raise it. Predict whether the shadow will lengthen or shorten, then move the height slowly. Pause to compare the ground. Follow the drawn ray past the top of the puppet: the shadow boundary changes with the light’s direction.

Now keep the height steady and move the lamp slowly from the puppet’s left to its right. The shadow shortens, then stretches out on the opposite side. The puppet has not turned around. A shadow depends on where light comes from and where it lands, as well as the object itself. Pause and look from above. It is still the same shadow, seen from another viewpoint.

A real lamp is not a single point, so shadow edges can look soft. The Sun makes shadows too, but it is very far away and differs from a tabletop lamp. Ask an adult to investigate with a flashlight and block, and explain your discoveries. Watch the surface, without staring into a strong light.
