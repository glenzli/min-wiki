Is anything changing inside a pupa that appears still?

Select egg, larva, pupa and adult in order. Open the inside view at the pupa stage and compare larval and adult structures: these are stages of one individual. Select the adult and play wing preparation. Compare crumpled and expanded wings; pause, scrub or look closer at the wing veins.

## Complete metamorphosis separates life-stage roles

Butterflies pass through egg, larva, pupa and adult. Larvae feed and accumulate material for growth and later development. Their cuticle requires repeated moulting as they enlarge. Adult structures support dispersal and reproduction. Different diets and habitats do not make the caterpillar a separate kind of individual.

## The pupa undergoes organised remodelling

Pupation does not turn every part into an unstructured liquid. Some larval tissues break down or remodel while adult tissues develop under hormonal control. Reduced external movement is not an absence of metabolism. The inside view simplifies a coordinated developmental process rather than depicting every tissue or its exact timing.

## Emergence begins another transition

After emergence, wings must expand and become flight-ready. Returning to an egg in the life-cycle diagram means reproduction and the next generation, not an adult becoming its own egg. Timing varies with species, temperature and diapause. Host plants are often specific; monarch larvae, for example, depend on milkweeds. Fluid entering the wing veins helps the soft wings expand before they harden. The size changes illustrate expansion, not exact folding geometry or species-specific timing.

## A common misconception

A pupa is neither a sleeping caterpillar nor a butterfly egg; it is a distinct, active developmental stage.

## Model limits

Stages are representative and use different size and time scales. The internal view explains development and is not a literal anatomical section.

### Begin with an egg

A tiny egg rests on a leaf, with a new life developing inside. Many butterflies lay eggs on plants their young can eat. What hatches is not a miniature winged butterfly but a caterpillar. What do you think this young animal will spend much of its time doing?

*Scene cue: Show the egg, then the larva.*

### Eat and grow

The caterpillar feeds, grows and sheds a skin that has become too small. It is the butterfly in its young stage, not another animal taking its place. Eventually it becomes a pupa. Pause and inspect the quiet pupa: does being still outside mean nothing is happening inside?

*Scene cue: Move from larva to pupa and pause for three seconds.*

### A quiet outside, a busy inside

Open the inside view. The body is developing: some structures change and others keep growing, including those needed by the adult. Our drawing helps us imagine an otherwise hidden process, but a real body is much more complex. Wings do not simply appear by magic after a long sleep.

*Scene cue: Reveal the developing adult structures inside the pupa.*

### Prepare the wings before flying

At last the adult emerges. Its wings still need to expand and become ready for flight. Later, adults may produce eggs and a new generation begins. Trace the sequence with your finger: egg, caterpillar, pupa, butterfly. The next egg belongs to its offspring; the adult does not turn back into its own egg.

*Scene cue: Play adult wing preparation; pause at crumpled, expanded and hardening observations, then review the four stages.*
