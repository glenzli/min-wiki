Blood looks red, but it contains more than one kind of thing. Imagine bringing a magnifying glass to a small blood vessel. The little discs with thinner middles and thicker rims are red cells. There are also different white cells, tiny platelets, and the plasma around them. Let us look at their jobs separately.

Red cells contain hemoglobin, which can bind oxygen. They pick up oxygen near the lungs and travel with the blood. Advance the picture slowly. Gold markers leave the red cell and reach nearby tissue. Oxygen leaves; the red cell stays inside the vessel. Real oxygen molecules are far too small to see as these gold dots.

Change the scene. This white cell, with its lobed nucleus, is a neutrophil. It can change shape, cross the vessel wall and engulf some bacteria in the tissue. More processing is needed afterward. White cells come in many kinds, and they do not all work in this way. Protecting the body takes many cooperating partners.

Finally, look at a small break. Tiny platelets stick and gather, and fibrin forms a network that helps stabilize the local clot. Platelets are actually little fragments produced by larger cells. Carrying oxygen, protecting tissues and helping stop bleeding are different jobs. Can you point to each participant and describe what it did?
